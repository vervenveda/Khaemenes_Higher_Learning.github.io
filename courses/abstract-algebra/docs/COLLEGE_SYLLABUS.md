# Khaemenes Higher Learning Abstract Algebra — Advanced Syllabus

**Length:** 36 weeks · 180 sessions  
**Lessons:** 92 interactive lessons and 92 faculty keys  
**Author:** Jennifer Pearl

## Academic level

An advanced upper-level and graduate-preparatory abstract algebra sequence with selected beginning-graduate topics. It is not represented as a replacement for a university graduate algebra qualifying sequence.

## Prerequisite

Introduction to Proofs or equivalent proof fluency is required. Linear Algebra and Discrete Mathematics are strongly recommended; number-theory familiarity is helpful

## Course outcomes

- Define algebraic structures, operations, substructures, morphisms, quotients, extensions, and universal properties precisely.
- Construct rigorous proofs about groups, rings, fields, modules, actions, ideals, extensions, and canonical decompositions.
- Use proof, modular arithmetic, linear algebra, polynomial reasoning, and structure-preserving maps precisely.
- Design examples, counterexamples, subgroup and ideal lattices, field towers, commutative diagrams, and proof revisions.
- Write, cite, peer-review, compute, and orally defend theorem-level algebraic research.

## Unit sequence

1. **Algebraic Structures, Operations & Proof Foundations** — Weeks 1–3
2. **Groups, Subgroups & Cyclic Structure** — Weeks 4–6
3. **Cosets, Lagrange Theory & Quotient Counting** — Weeks 7–9
4. **Permutation Groups, Group Actions & Symmetry Counting** — Weeks 10–12
5. **Homomorphisms, Normal Subgroups & Quotient Groups** — Weeks 13–15
6. **Finite Groups, Sylow Theory & Abelian Classification** — Weeks 16–18
7. **Rings, Ideals & Quotient Rings** — Weeks 19–21
8. **Domains, Factorization & Polynomial Rings** — Weeks 22–24
9. **Fields, Extensions & Finite Fields** — Weeks 25–27
10. **Galois Theory & Polynomial Solvability** — Weeks 28–30
11. **Modules, Canonical Forms & Representation Foundations** — Weeks 31–32
12. **Applications: Coding, Cryptography, Symmetry & Computation** — Weeks 33–34
13. **Advanced Algebra, Research Methods & Capstone** — Weeks 35–36

## Evidence model

The course includes 13 investigations, 13 mastery checks, a 40-question diagnostic, 60-question midterm, 100-question final, portfolio, independent research capstone, reproducible appendix, and oral defense. Corrections and proof revision are required components of mastery.

## Technology and integrity

Software, calculators, proof assistants, plotting, numerical experiments, and AI may support exploration when disclosed. Learners remain responsible for verifying every theorem, source, proof, calculation, and citation and must be able to defend submitted work independently.
