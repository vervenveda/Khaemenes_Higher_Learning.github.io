Faculty solution guide

Cayley’s Theorem

Accept equivalent mathematically correct reasoning. Require explicit definitions, theorem hypotheses, proof structure, verification, and limitations.

Problem 1

Use the precise definitions, verify closure and all theorem hypotheses, apply G≅subgroup of S_G, and test the conclusion on a nontrivial example and a boundary or counterexample case.

Problem 2

Identify the first invalid inference, missing hypothesis, quantifier error, unjustified limit interchange, or unproved existence claim; then supply a valid repair.

Problem 3

A complete response explicitly states the definitions, domain, hypotheses, exact target, and theorem dependencies for Cayley’s Theorem.

Problem 4

The argument must address embedding every group into a permutation group with each nontrivial step justified and the final conclusion restated.

Problem 5

A strong application explains the mathematical model in molecular symmetry, combinatorial enumeration, graph automorphisms, puzzles, pattern design, and state-space reduction, verifies conditions, and names at least one limitation.

Problem 6

Accept a second valid proof, a counterexample showing necessity of a hypothesis, an independent estimate, or a reproducible finite check that is correctly described as supporting evidence.
