# Advanced Geometric Topology — Course Master Plan

## Existing 36-week spine

| Weeks | Unit | Curriculum focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Classification of Surfaces & Low-Dimensional Topology | Quotients, classification, groups, homology, orientability |
| 4–6 | Knot & Link Theory | Knots, links, isotopy, Reidemeister moves, invariants, braids |
| 7–9 | Three-Manifolds & Geometric Topology | Handlebodies, Heegaard splittings, surgery, JSJ, hyperbolic geometry |
| 10–12 | Computational Topology & Persistent Homology | Complexes, chains, filtrations, persistence, TDA |
| 13–15 | Morse Theory & Handle Decompositions | Critical points, Hessian, index, handles, Morse inequalities |
| 16–18 | Geometric Flows | Curve shortening, mean curvature, Ricci flow, singularities |
| 19–21 | Global Riemannian Geometry, Comparison & Rigidity | Jacobi fields, conjugate points, comparison, rigidity |
| 22–24 | Geometry of Relativity | Lorentzian geometry, causality, curvature, Schwarzschild geometry |
| 25–27 | Gauge Geometry, Connections & Holonomy | Bundles, connections, curvature, holonomy, Yang–Mills foundations |
| 28–29 | Characteristic Classes & Global Bundle Invariants | Euler, Chern, Stiefel–Whitney, Chern–Weil |
| 30–31 | Geometry, Physics & Modern Applications | Mechanics, robotics, optimization, topological phases, geometric learning |
| 32–33 | Advanced Mathematical Research Methods | Literature, theorem dependency, reproducibility |
| 34–36 | Research Capstone, Synthesis & Oral Defense | Research build, revision, reproduction, defense |

## Proposed curriculum-wide improvement

Every week should deliberately move through a progression such as:

1. Foundations — definitions, intuition, examples
2. Structure — propositions, theorem relationships
3. Proof — guided to independent argument
4. Construction / computation — build and test
5. Synthesis — counterexample, application, reflection, assessment

This is a planning target, not yet a mandate for every existing session.

## Proposed prerequisite diagnostic

Before Week 1, assess:

- topology
- linear algebra
- calculus / analysis
- differential geometry
- proof methods
- mathematical maturity

The diagnostic should identify repair needs rather than simply reject a learner.

## Proposed mastery standard

A strong completion model should require appropriate evidence across:

- conceptual understanding
- mathematical problem solving
- proof or proof critique
- construction / computation where appropriate
- laboratory interpretation where assigned
- synthesis
- explanation or defense

A numerical score alone should not certify proof mastery.

## Proposed mathematical narrative

Topology → knots → 3-manifolds → computational topology → Morse theory → evolving geometry → global geometry → Lorentzian geometry → gauge geometry → characteristic classes → applications → research.

The goal is to make these transitions explicit so the course feels like one developing mathematical story rather than a list of advanced subjects.
