Edge Identification Proof Workshop

Advanced Geometric Topology · Self-Paced Lesson 04 · Arc 01

Resource ID: KH-MATH-AGT-W01-PW01 (legacy stable ID)

Mode: Guided proof construction → independent proof → proof audit

1. Purpose

You have learned the quotient construction and studied complete identification audits. This workshop transfers responsibility for the argument to you.

state the data → state the target → justify every passage → audit the conclusion

2. Proof Toolkit

  • equivalence relations and transitive closure;
  • the quotient map q:X→X/~;
  • endpoint correspondence induced by paired edges;
  • vertex, edge, and face equivalence classes;
  • the local Euclidean condition for surfaces;
  • homeomorphism as a continuous bijection with continuous inverse;
  • Euler characteristic χ=V−E+F once an appropriate finite cell structure is established.
Proof boundary: diagrams, folded-paper models, numerical experiments, and calculator output can support conjecture or intuition; they do not replace the required topological argument.

3. Workshop A — Torus Vertex Identification

Given: a b a−1 b−1, with square vertices v0,v1,v2,v3 in boundary order.

Show: all four polygon vertices determine one quotient vertex.

A1 · Independent attempt

Write the endpoint relations generated by the a-pair and b-pair. Do not use the name “torus” as a substitute for the calculation.

Hint 1

Treat each edge pairing as a map between intervals and track corresponding initial and terminal endpoints.

Hint 2

One pair gives v0~v3 and v1~v2; the other connects those groups.

A2 · Proof skeleton

  1. State the a-pair relations.
  2. State the b-pair relations.
  3. Use symmetry/transitivity.
  4. Conclude [v0]=[v1]=[v2]=[v3].

A3 · Model proof

The a identification gives v0~v3 and v1~v2. The b identification gives v1~v0 and v2~v3. Since v1~v0 and v0~v3, transitivity gives v1~v3. Together with v1~v2, every vertex is equivalent to v1. Thus the four corners determine one quotient vertex.

A4 · Audit

  • Where was transitivity used?
  • Why would counting four drawn corners be wrong?
  • Which relations depend on the orientation convention?

4. Workshop B — Torus Cell Count

Show: (V,E,F)=(1,2,1), hence χ=0.

Required argument: explain every count. Bare substitution into V−E+F is not a proof of the cell structure.

Workshop A gives V=1. The four boundary edges form two paired quotient edge classes, a and b, so E=2. The polygon interior supplies one 2-cell, so F=1. Therefore χ=1−2+1=0.

5. Workshop C — Same χ, Different Surface

Claim: “If two closed connected surfaces have the same Euler characteristic, then they are homeomorphic.”

Task: disprove the claim using established Arc 01 benchmarks.

Hint

Compare the torus and Klein bottle.

Model counterexample: both benchmark cell structures have V=1,E=2,F=1, hence χ=0. The torus is orientable and the Klein bottle is nonorientable. Since orientability is preserved by homeomorphism, they cannot be homeomorphic.

Logical lesson: differing values of an invariant can obstruct homeomorphism; equal values need not establish it.

6. Workshop D — Replace “It Looks Like” with an Argument

Rewrite “gluing opposite sides makes a torus” so that your argument distinguishes:

  • the square I×I;
  • the equivalence relation;
  • the quotient map;
  • the benchmark torus model;
  • the theorem or explicit construction used to establish the homeomorphism.

Audit: label which sentences construct the quotient and which justify its identification with the benchmark torus.

7. Workshop E — Local Surface Proof Strategy

For a polygonal quotient, organize the local audit by quotient-point type.

Point typePreimageRequired neighborhood argument
Face pointInterior of polygonRetains an open-disk neighborhood.
Edge pointInteriors of a paired edge pairTwo half-disk neighborhoods assemble into a disk.
Vertex pointOne or more identified cornersIncident sectors assemble cyclically into a disk, rather than a branch or other singular neighborhood.

Task: write a proof outline using all three cases. Do not claim that every arbitrary boundary equivalence automatically satisfies the vertex case.

Later connection: stars and links will eventually make this local audit combinatorial.

8. Workshop F — Find the Hidden Gaps

“The arrows match and the picture folds into a familiar object. Therefore the quotient is homeomorphic to that object.”

Identify at least three gaps. Your critique should address the equivalence relation, quotient topology/local neighborhoods, and the missing homeomorphism argument.

9. Workshop G — Repair an Overclaim

Observed: an unfamiliar polygonal quotient has χ=−2.

Overclaim: “Therefore it is the orientable genus-two surface.”

  1. State exactly what χ=−2 establishes.
  2. Identify additional properties to investigate, including orientability and the surface hypotheses.
  3. Name the type of classification result that could eventually justify a definitive identification.
  4. Explain why that theorem cannot be silently imported into a proof that has not established its hypotheses or authorized its use.

10. Independent Proof Challenge — Projective Plane

For the benchmark word a a:

  1. derive the endpoint relations;
  2. prove that the polygon vertices form one quotient vertex class;
  3. justify V=1,E=1,F=1;
  4. compute χ=1;
  5. explain the orientation-reversing behavior represented by the pairing;
  6. separate direct calculations from established benchmark facts.

No model proof is included here. Preserve this task as an independent proof before consulting later solution/remediation resources.

11. Proof-Writing Rubric

DimensionMastery evidence
Statement controlGiven data and proof target are explicit.
DefinitionsDefinitions are used correctly, not merely named.
Identification logicEndpoint relations and equivalence classes are justified.
InferenceNontrivial steps have reasons.
Topology vs pictureVisualization is not presented as proof.
Invariant logicNecessary and sufficient implications are not confused.
ScopeThe conclusion is no stronger than the hypotheses/results permit.
RevisionDetected gaps are repaired explicitly.

12. Final Proof Audit

  • I stated what is given.
  • I stated what must be shown.
  • I correctly used every important definition.
  • I translated gluing instructions into endpoint/equivalence relations.
  • I counted quotient cells after identifications.
  • I did not treat a picture or calculation as proof of a stronger claim.
  • I checked implication direction when using invariants.
  • I identified theorems or benchmark results I relied upon.
  • I checked theorem hypotheses before invoking a conclusion.
  • My final sentence answers the exact proof target.

13. Accessibility

All proof tasks are stated textually and do not require color. Any diagram must be accompanied by its boundary word, vertex labels, edge directions, and endpoint correspondences. No external website, paid software, or physical model is required.
