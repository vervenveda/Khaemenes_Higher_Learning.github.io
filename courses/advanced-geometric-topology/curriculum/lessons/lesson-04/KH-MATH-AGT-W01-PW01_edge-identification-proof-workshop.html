Edge Identification Proof Workshop

Advanced Geometric Topology · Self-Paced Lesson 04

Resource ID: KH-MATH-AGT-W01-PW01

Mode: Guided proof construction → independent proof → proof audit

1. Purpose

You have learned the quotient construction and watched complete identification audits. This workshop changes your role: you must now build, test, and revise mathematical arguments.

The central habit is simple: state exactly what is known, exactly what must be shown, and justify the passage between them.

2. Proof Toolkit

  • equivalence relations and transitive closure;
  • the quotient map q:X→X/~;
  • endpoint correspondence induced by paired edges;
  • vertex, edge, and face equivalence classes;
  • the local Euclidean condition for surfaces;
  • homeomorphism as a continuous bijection with continuous inverse;
  • Euler characteristic χ=V−E+F as an invariant once an appropriate finite cell structure is established.

Proof boundary: a diagram, folded paper model, numerical experiment, or calculator output can support a conjecture, but it is not a substitute for the required topological argument.

3. Workshop A — Prove the Torus Vertex Identification

Given: the square boundary word a b a−1 b−1, with vertices v0,v1,v2,v3 in boundary order.

Show: all four polygon vertices lie in one equivalence class in the quotient.

A1. Independent attempt

Write the endpoint relations generated by the a-pair and b-pair. Do not name the torus yet.

Hint 1

Treat each paired edge as a map between intervals. Which initial endpoint corresponds to which initial or terminal endpoint under the orientation rule?

Hint 2

You should obtain relations linking v0 to v3 and v1 to v2 from one pair, then relations linking the two groups from the other pair.

A2. Proof skeleton

  1. State the endpoint relations generated by the a identification.
  2. State the endpoint relations generated by the b identification.
  3. Use symmetry/transitivity of the generated equivalence relation.
  4. Conclude [v0]=[v1]=[v2]=[v3].

A3. Model proof

The a-edge identification gives v0~v3 and v1~v2. The b-edge identification gives v1~v0 and v2~v3. Since v1~v0 and v0~v3, transitivity gives v1~v3. Together with v1~v2, every vertex is equivalent to v1. Hence all four corners determine a single quotient vertex.

A4. Audit

  • Where was transitivity used?
  • Would counting the four drawn corners have proved the result?
  • Which part of the argument depends on edge direction?

4. Workshop B — Prove the Torus Cell Count

Given: the same standard square presentation.

Show: its quotient cell structure has (V,E,F)=(1,2,1), and therefore χ=0.

Required argument: explain each count. A bare substitution into V−E+F earns no proof credit.

Least-support hint

Ask what the equivalence classes of 0-cells and 1-cells are after the quotient.

Model structure

Workshop A established one vertex class, so V=1. The four boundary edges occur in two paired classes, a and b, so E=2. The interior of the polygon is not identified with another 2-cell, so it supplies one face, F=1. Therefore χ=1−2+1=0.

5. Workshop C — Same χ, Different Surface

Claim to evaluate: “If two closed connected surfaces have the same Euler characteristic, then they are homeomorphic.”

Task: disprove the claim using material established in Lessons 01–03.

Hint

Compare the torus and Klein bottle.

Model counterexample argument: the standard torus and Klein-bottle cell structures both have χ=0. The torus is orientable and the Klein bottle is nonorientable. Since orientability is preserved by homeomorphism, they cannot be homeomorphic. Therefore equal Euler characteristic does not imply homeomorphism.

6. Workshop D — Prove a Quotient Claim Without “It Looks Like”

Task: rewrite the informal statement “gluing opposite sides makes a torus” as a mathematically responsible argument.

  • distinguish the square I×I;
  • state the equivalence relation on boundary points;
  • identify the quotient map;
  • identify the benchmark torus model to which the quotient is compared.

Challenge: identify which part of your argument is a construction and which part establishes homeomorphism to the benchmark torus.

7. Workshop E — Local Surface Proof Strategy

Let P be a polygonal region whose edges are paired by suitable edge homeomorphisms. To justify that P/~ is a surface, organize the local argument by quotient-point type.

Point typePreimage locationNeighborhood question
Face pointInterior of PWhy does it retain an open-disk neighborhood?
Edge pointInterior points of a paired edge pairWhy do two half-disk neighborhoods join to form a disk?
Vertex pointOne or more identified polygon verticesWhy do the incident sectors assemble into a disk rather than a branch or singularity?

Task: write a proof outline using these three cases. Do not claim more than the pairing hypotheses justify.

8. Workshop F — Find the Hidden Gap

“The arrows match and the picture folds into a familiar object. Therefore the quotient is homeomorphic to that object.”

Identify at least three gaps. Your critique should consider the quotient definition, local neighborhoods, and the actual justification for a homeomorphism.

9. Workshop G — Repair an Overclaim

Observed: an unfamiliar polygonal quotient has χ=−2.

Overclaim: “Therefore it is the orientable genus-two surface.”

  1. State what χ=−2 actually establishes.
  2. State what additional information you would investigate.
  3. Name the classification result that would eventually justify a definitive identification.
  4. Explain why the evidence established so far does not authorize silently invoking that theorem.

10. Independent Proof Challenge

For the standard projective-plane word a a:

  1. define the endpoint relations carefully;
  2. prove that the polygon vertices form one quotient vertex class;
  3. justify V=1, E=1, F=1;
  4. compute χ;
  5. explain which feature of the identification signals nonorientable behavior;
  6. separate the parts you proved from facts used as established benchmark results.

No model proof appears here. The later solution-commentary lesson provides comparison after an independent attempt.

11. Proof-Writing Rubric

DimensionMastery evidence
Statement controlGiven data and proof target are explicit.
DefinitionsRelevant definitions are used correctly rather than merely named.
Identification logicEndpoint relations and equivalence classes are justified.
InferenceNontrivial steps have reasons.
Topology vs pictureVisualization is not presented as proof.
Invariant logicNecessary and sufficient implications are not confused.
ScopeThe conclusion is no stronger than the established hypotheses/results allow.
RevisionDetected gaps are repaired explicitly.

12. Final Proof Audit

  • I stated what is given.
  • I stated what must be shown.
  • I defined or correctly invoked every important term.
  • I translated gluing instructions into equivalence relations.
  • I justified quotient cell counts after identifications.
  • I did not treat a picture, experiment, or calculation as proof of a stronger claim.
  • I checked implication direction involving invariants.
  • I identified any theorem I relied upon.
  • My final sentence answers the exact proof target.

13. Accessibility

All proof tasks are stated textually and do not require color. Any later diagram must include its boundary word, vertex labels, edge directions, and endpoint correspondences. Mathematical symbols should be readable in words when assistive technology is used.
