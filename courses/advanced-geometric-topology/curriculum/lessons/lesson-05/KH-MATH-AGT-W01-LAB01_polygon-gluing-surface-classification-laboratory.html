Laboratory Purpose

This laboratory turns the quotient-space methods of Lessons 01–04 into reproducible investigations. You will make predictions, compute identifications, test invariants, audit local topology, and distinguish experimental evidence from proof.

Laboratory rule: a drawing, folded model, or numerical pattern may suggest a conclusion. Your submitted conclusion must be supported by symbolic quotient data and an appropriate argument.

Lab Record Protocol

For every investigation record: boundary word; polygon vertices in boundary order; paired edges and direction; endpoint relations; transitive vertex classes; quotient V,E,F; χ; orientability evidence; local-surface audit when needed; conjecture; final justified conclusion; and whether each conclusion is direct, benchmark-based, or theorem-dependent.

Investigation 1 — Torus Control

Input: a b a^-1 b^-1.

  1. Predict the quotient before calculating.
  2. Derive every endpoint relation.
  3. Compute vertex classes and (V,E,F).
  4. Compute χ.
  5. Explain why this reproduces the Arc 01 torus benchmark.

Investigation 2 — Direction Changes the Quotient

Compare the 2-gon presentations a a and a a^-1.

  1. Compute endpoint relations separately.
  2. Compare quotient vertex counts.
  3. Compute χ for both.
  4. Identify the established benchmark surface for each.
  5. State exactly what changed in the gluing data.

Investigation 3 — Torus vs Klein Bottle

Compare a b a^-1 b^-1 with a b a b^-1.

  1. Compute quotient cells for both.
  2. Verify that both have χ=0.
  3. Compare orientation behavior.
  4. Explain why equal Euler characteristic does not establish homeomorphism.

Investigation 4 — Presentation Simplification

Input: a b b^-1 a^-1.

  1. Mark the adjacent inverse pair.
  2. Explain the cancellation in the standard polygonal-presentation calculus.
  3. Determine the remaining benchmark word.
  4. Compute the expected χ and compare it with the sphere benchmark.
  5. Explain why word length is not a topological invariant.

Investigation 5 — Genus-Two Control

Input: a b a^-1 b^-1 c d c^-1 d^-1.

  1. Determine the number of quotient edge classes.
  2. Audit the endpoint relations and vertex classes.
  3. Compute (V,E,F) and χ.
  4. Identify the commutator-block pattern.
  5. Separate what you directly computed from what relies on the established genus-two benchmark.

Investigation 6 — Local-Manifold Stress Test

Construct or describe a boundary identification that you suspect creates a singular quotient point rather than a surface point.

  1. Name the suspicious quotient point p.
  2. Describe q^-1(p).
  3. Analyze a small neighborhood after identification.
  4. Decide whether it is locally Euclidean.
  5. Record the evidence that confirms or rejects the surface claim.

Constraint: do not use Euler characteristic as a substitute for the local-neighborhood audit.

Investigation 7 — Blind Classification Audit

Choose an unfamiliar paired-edge word supplied by the instructor/course bank. Do not begin by naming a surface.

  1. Run the full identification audit.
  2. Compute χ.
  3. Investigate orientability.
  4. Verify the surface hypotheses.
  5. List all conclusions justified without a classification theorem.
  6. If a classification theorem is later invoked, state its hypotheses explicitly.

Laboratory Reflection

  1. Which quotient conclusion was least obvious from the original polygon?
  2. Where did transitive closure matter most?
  3. Give one example in which χ was useful but insufficient.
  4. How did orientation data add information?
  5. Why must local topology be checked independently of global cell counts?
  6. What is the difference between experimental confirmation and proof in this laboratory?

Mastery Gate

  • Complete at least Investigations 1–6.
  • Produce reproducible endpoint-equivalence work.
  • Correctly distinguish polygon cells from quotient cells.
  • Use χ with correct logical direction.
  • Distinguish edge-direction data from global orientability.
  • Perform a local-surface audit where required.
  • Label direct verification versus benchmark/theorem use.

Accessibility & Sovereignty

Every investigation is fully expressible with text and symbols. Physical folding is optional. Color is never required. No external website, paid software, remote API, or network connection is required. The laboratory can be printed and completed entirely offline.
