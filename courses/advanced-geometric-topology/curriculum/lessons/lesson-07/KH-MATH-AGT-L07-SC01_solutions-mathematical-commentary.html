How to Use This Lesson

Attempt Lesson 06 before consulting this commentary. Compare your reasoning, not merely your final answer. When your solution differs, locate the first step at which the mathematical logic diverges.

For proof questions, a correct final statement does not repair a missing argument. For computational questions, correct arithmetic does not repair incorrect quotient-cell counts.

Part A — Foundations

1. Equivalence relations and partitions

An equivalence relation ~ on X is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive. For x∈X, define [x]={y∈X:y~x}. Reflexivity gives x∈[x], so the classes cover X. If [x]∩[y]≠∅, choose z in the intersection. Then x~z and y~z; symmetry gives z~y, so transitivity gives x~y. If u∈[x], then u~x~y, hence u∈[y]. Thus [x]⊆[y], and the reverse inclusion is symmetric. Therefore intersecting classes are equal, so the equivalence classes partition X.

Common error: listing the three axioms without proving the partition claim.

2. Quotient map

Let C∈X/~. By definition C=[x] for some x∈X. Since q(x)=[x]=C, every element of X/~ has a preimage. Therefore q is surjective.

Proof habit: for surjectivity, begin with an arbitrary element of the codomain.

3. Quotient topology

The quotient topology on X/~ is defined by declaring U⊆X/~ open exactly when q−1(U) is open in X, where q:X→X/~ is the quotient map.

4. Presentation versus object

The polygonal region records identification instructions. The quotient consists of equivalence classes of polygon points. Several polygon points may become one quotient point, and different polygonal presentations may describe homeomorphic quotient spaces. Therefore the presentation and the resulting surface must not be identified literally.

5. Local condition

Under the convention used in Arc 01, a surface without boundary is locally Euclidean of dimension two: each point has a neighborhood homeomorphic to an open subset of R²; after suitable shrinking, one may use an open disk as the local model.

Part B — Edge and Vertex Audits

6. Standard torus

For a b a−1 b−1, label the square vertices v0,v1,v2,v3 in boundary order. Under the course convention, the a-pair gives v0~v3 and v1~v2; the b-pair gives v1~v0 and v2~v3. Transitivity gives one class [v0]=[v1]=[v2]=[v3].

7. The word a a

The same-sign pairing identifies the two polygon vertices, so V=1. There is one quotient edge and one face: E=1,F=1. Thus

χ=1−1+1=1.

This is the Arc 01 benchmark presentation of RP².

8. The word a a−1

The opposite-sign endpoint correspondence leaves two quotient vertex classes. Hence V=2,E=1,F=1 and

χ=2−1+1=2.

This is the Arc 01 benchmark 2-gon presentation of S². Problems 7 and 8 show that repeated labels alone do not determine endpoint correspondence.

9. Klein-bottle word

For a b a b−1, the a-pair gives v0~v2 and v1~v3, while the b-pair gives v1~v0 and v2~v3. All four vertices lie in one equivalence class. Therefore V=1,E=2,F=1 and χ=0.

10. Same number of sides

The words a a and a a−1 both use a 2-gon but produce different endpoint-equivalence structures, different vertex counts, different Euler characteristics, and different benchmark surfaces. Polygon side count is therefore not enough to determine homeomorphism type.

Part C — Invariants and Logic

11. Torus Euler characteristic

After quotienting, the standard torus cell structure has V=1,E=2,F=1. Therefore χ=1−2+1=0.

12. Equal χ is insufficient

Homeomorphic finite-CW surfaces have the same Euler characteristic, but the converse need not hold. The torus and Klein bottle both have χ=0, while the torus is orientable and the Klein bottle is nonorientable. Thus χ alone does not distinguish them.

13. Contrapositive

If two such surfaces have different Euler characteristics, then they are not homeomorphic.

Logic note: this is the contrapositive of the invariant statement, not its converse.

14. Invariant versus complete invariant

An invariant takes the same value on homeomorphic objects. A complete invariant for a specified class also distinguishes equivalence classes: equality of the invariant is sufficient for equivalence within that class. Euler characteristic is an invariant but is not by itself a complete invariant for the closed surfaces encountered here.

15. Counterexample

T² and the Klein bottle provide the counterexample: both have χ=0, but orientability distinguishes them. Therefore equal Euler characteristic does not imply homeomorphism.

Part D — Higher-Complexity Presentations

16. Orientable genus-two presentation

For a b a−1 b−1 c d c−1 d−1, the standard endpoint relations place all eight polygon vertices into one equivalence class. The labels a,b,c,d give four quotient edges, and the polygon interior gives one face. Thus

(V,E,F)=(1,4,1), χ=1−4+1=−2.

Method requirement: this result comes from quotient-cell analysis, not from substituting genus into a formula excluded by the prompt.

17. Why “eight sides” is not classification

Side count belongs to the chosen presentation and is not a homeomorphism invariant. The identifications determine the quotient structure.

18. Expanded sphere presentation

The words a a−1 and a b b−1 a−1 illustrate the standard elementary adjacent inverse-pair simplification: the neighboring b b−1 pair can be removed in the polygonal-presentation calculus, leaving a a−1. Thus presentation length is not a topological invariant.

19. Student-created word

Answers vary. A complete answer states the convention, uses each edge label exactly twice, derives endpoint relations, forms vertex equivalence classes, determines quotient edge classes and the face count, and computes χ. A surface name is optional unless justified by an established benchmark, direct homeomorphism argument, or authorized theorem.

20. Limits of inference

A valid answer reports the verified quotient data and any properties established from Arc 01 methods. It must not silently import the full surface-classification theorem. A stronger name/classification requires a theorem whose hypotheses have been checked or a direct argument sufficient to establish the claimed homeomorphism type.

Part E — Proof and Forensics

21. One torus vertex class

The a-identification gives v0~v3 and v1~v2. The b-identification gives v1~v0 and v2~v3. Since v1~v0 and v0~v3, transitivity gives v1~v3; together with v1~v2, all four vertices lie in one class. Hence the quotient has V=1.

22. Faulty raw-polygon count

The error is counting cells before identification. In the torus quotient, four corners form one quotient vertex class and four boundary sides form two quotient edge classes; the interior remains one face. Therefore χ=1−2+1=0.

23. Missing orientation data

The label a tells us which edges are paired, but not by itself which endpoints correspond. The words a a and a a−1 encode different directional relations under the course convention; their endpoint equivalence classes differ, so the quotients differ.

24. Local forensics

Let p be the suspicious quotient point. Determine q−1(p), choose sufficiently small neighborhoods of those preimage points, and analyze how the quotient identification joins those neighborhood pieces. For p to be a surface point without boundary in this course's convention, it must have a neighborhood homeomorphic to an open subset of R². Branching, pinching, or another non-disk-like local structure demonstrates failure of the condition.

25. Evidence boundary

Established: if the quotient-cell audit is correct, χ=−1. If the orientation analysis is also valid, nonorientability has been established by the methods available in the course.

Not yet automatic: a unique homeomorphism classification from those invariants. That step generally uses a classification theorem for compact connected surfaces (with its hypotheses verified) or another sufficient result. Until such a theorem is authorized, report the invariants without pretending they constitute a complete classification proof.

27. Separated / not separated

S² and T² are separated by Euler characteristic because 2≠0. T² and the Klein bottle are not separated by Euler characteristic because both have χ=0.

28. Orientability adds information

Euler characteristic is numerical. Orientability records whether local orientations can be chosen consistently across the entire surface. Since T² and the Klein bottle share χ but differ in orientability, orientability carries information not contained in χ alone.

29. Audit algorithm

State the convention; identify edge pairs and directions; derive endpoint relations; take transitive closure; count quotient vertices, edges, and faces; compute χ; inspect orientation behavior; check local Euclidean neighborhoods when necessary; then state only the classification conclusion supported by established results.

30. Capstone commentary

Answers vary. A strong response explains that the raw polygon is presentation-dependent, while the quotient construction organizes points into equivalence classes and produces the topological object under study. It should use at least two concrete examples. The torus and Klein bottle are especially useful because both have χ=0 but differ in orientability, showing that Euler characteristic can carry genuine information without being a complete classifier.

The response should also distinguish endpoint correspondence, quotient-cell counts, local manifold structure, orientability, and theorem use. The strongest answers explicitly identify which conclusions were calculated directly from the presentation and which depend on established benchmark identifications or later classification results.
