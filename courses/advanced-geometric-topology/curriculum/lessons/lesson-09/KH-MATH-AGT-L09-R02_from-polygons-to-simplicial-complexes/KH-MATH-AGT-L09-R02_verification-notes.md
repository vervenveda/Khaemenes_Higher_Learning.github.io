# Lesson 09 Verification Notes

## Mathematical checks
The lesson uses the standard abstract-complex face-closure definition and the standard geometric-complex condition that intersections of simplices are common faces.

A deliberate distinction is made between ordinary simplicial complexes and Δ-complexes. This is important because the familiar square-with-diagonal torus quotient gives a two-triangle Δ-complex, not a genuine two-triangle simplicial-complex triangulation.

## Editorial verification
Cornell materials verify:
- simplices as basic dimensional building blocks;
- abstract simplicial complexes as face-closed collections;
- geometric realization;
- geometric complexes meeting along common faces.

Hatcher's Algebraic Topology discussion verifies the pedagogically important torus distinction: the square with a diagonal gives a two-triangle Δ-complex structure, whereas an ordinary simplicial-complex triangulation requires more simplices.

External references verify mathematics only. They are not required learner dependencies.

## Scope boundary
Lesson 09 introduces edge-incidence evidence for boundary/nonmanifold behavior but intentionally postpones the full vertex-local criterion to Lesson 10 (stars and links).
