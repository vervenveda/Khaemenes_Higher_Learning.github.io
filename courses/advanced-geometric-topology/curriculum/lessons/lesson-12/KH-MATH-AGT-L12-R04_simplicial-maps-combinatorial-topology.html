Simplicial Maps & Combinatorial Topology

Advanced Geometric Topology · Self-Paced Lesson 12

Arc 02: Triangulations & Combinatorial Surfaces

Why Study Maps Combinatorially?

Lessons 09–11 converted spaces into simplicial data and showed that a triangulation may be refined without changing the underlying topology. We now turn from objects to maps.

A simplicial map is specified at the vertices, but the vertex assignment must respect simplices. When it does, the combinatorial rule extends naturally to a continuous map between geometric realizations.

vertex rule → simplex compatibility → linear realization → continuous map

1. Definition of a Simplicial Map

Let K and L be abstract simplicial complexes with vertex sets V(K) and V(L). A map

f:V(K)→V(L)

is simplicial if, for every simplex σ={v0,…,vk} of K, the image vertex set

f(σ)={f(v0),…,f(vk)}

is a simplex of L.

Repeated image vertices are allowed. Therefore a simplex may collapse to a lower-dimensional simplex.

2. Checking Only Facets in a Finite Complex

Because a simplicial complex is closed under taking nonempty faces, a vertex map on a finite complex is simplicial if it sends every facet of K to a simplex of L.

Every smaller simplex lies in a facet, and its image is then a face of the image of that facet.

Audit shortcut: for the finite complexes in this course, test the maximal simplices first.

3. A Valid Example

Let K have facets abc and acd. Let L contain the triangle xyz and all its faces. Define

f(a)=x, f(b)=y, f(c)=z, f(d)=y.

Then abc maps to xyz and acd maps to xzy, the same target triangle as a vertex set. Hence f is simplicial.

The fact that b and d have the same image does not violate the definition.

4. A Degenerate Image

Let abc be a triangle in K and suppose

f(a)=x, f(b)=y, f(c)=y.

Then the image of abc is the edge {x,y}. The 2-simplex has collapsed to a 1-simplex.

Simplicial maps are therefore not required to preserve dimension, injectivity, or distinct vertices.

5. An Invalid Vertex Rule

Suppose abc is a simplex of K and f(a)=x, f(b)=y, f(c)=z, but {x,y,z} is not a simplex of L.

Then f is not simplicial, even if each individual vertex and each pair happens to make sense elsewhere in L. The complete image of every source simplex must be a target simplex.

6. Geometric Realization of a Simplicial Map

A simplicial map f:K→L induces a continuous map

|f|:|K|→|L|.

On a point of a source simplex written in barycentric coordinates as

p=t0v0+⋯+tkvk, where ti≥0 and Σti=1, define

|f|(p)=t0f(v0)+⋯+tkf(vk).

This is affine-linear on each simplex. Because the formulas agree on shared faces, the simplexwise definitions fit together into a global continuous map.

7. Composition

If f:K→L and g:L→M are simplicial maps, then g∘f:K→M is simplicial.

Moreover, geometric realization respects composition:

|g∘f|=|g|∘|f|.

The identity vertex map is also simplicial. Thus simplicial complexes and simplicial maps form a category, and geometric realization converts these combinatorial maps into continuous maps.

8. Simplicial Isomorphism

A simplicial map f:K→L is a simplicial isomorphism if it has a simplicial inverse.

Equivalently for finite abstract complexes, it gives a bijection on vertices that preserves simplices in both directions.

A simplicial isomorphism induces a homeomorphism |K|→|L|. The converse is much stronger and is generally false for the original triangulations: homeomorphic realizations need not have identical combinatorial structures.

9. Worked Isomorphism Audit

Let K have facets abc and acd, and let L have facets pqr and prs. Define

a↦p, b↦q, c↦r, d↦s.

The facets map as abc↦pqr and acd↦prs. The vertex rule is bijective, and the inverse also preserves the two facets. Hence K and L are simplicially isomorphic.

10. A Simplicial Map Need Not Be a Homeomorphism

Take a triangle abc and send all three vertices to one vertex x. This is simplicial whenever {x} is a simplex of the target.

The realization collapses the entire triangle to a point. It is continuous, but it is not a homeomorphism.

Lesson: “simplicial” describes compatibility with the combinatorics; it does not imply invertibility.

11. Why Lesson 11 Matters

A continuous map between realizations need not be simplicial for the triangulations initially chosen. Subdivision gives us finer source triangulations and more vertices with which to encode the behavior of a continuous map.

This is the conceptual bridge to the simplicial approximation theorem.

12. Simplicial Approximation — Preview

For finite K, a standard form of the simplicial approximation theorem says that a continuous map

F:|K|→|L|

can, after sufficiently many barycentric subdivisions of K, be represented up to homotopy by the realization of a simplicial map.

Symbolically, for some n there is a simplicial map

g:sdnK→L   with   |g|≃F.

We are stating this as a preview, not yet proving it. The theorem explains why simplicial maps are powerful enough to study broad classes of continuous maps in algebraic topology.

13. Exact Approximation vs. Homotopy

The theorem does not generally say that the original continuous map F becomes literally equal to |g|. The conclusion is typically homotopic approximation.

That distinction matters. Algebraic topology often studies properties invariant under homotopy, so replacing F by a homotopic simplicial map can preserve the information of interest while making the map combinatorially computable.

14. A First Map of a Circle

Let K be the boundary of a triangle with vertices a,b,c and edges ab,bc,ca. Let L be another triangular circle with vertices x,y,z.

The assignment a↦x, b↦y, c↦z defines a simplicial isomorphism.

By contrast, a↦x, b↦x, c↦y defines a simplicial map only if each source edge maps to a vertex or to an edge present in L. This map collapses one edge and is not an isomorphism.

15. Image and Preimage Discipline

For a simplicial map, the image of a simplex is always a simplex, possibly of smaller dimension. However, the inverse image of a simplex need not itself be one simplex; it may be a union of simplices or a more complicated subcomplex-like collection.

Do not transfer ordinary set-map intuition into an unsupported combinatorial conclusion. Audit the simplices explicitly.

16. Local Behavior

A simplicial map can fold, collapse, or identify portions of a complex. Consequently, good manifold links in the source and target do not imply that the map is locally a homeomorphism.

To prove stronger map properties—such as injectivity, embedding behavior, or local homeomorphism—additional hypotheses are required.

17. Simplicial Map Audit

Given a proposed vertex map f:V(K)→V(L):

  1. List the facets of K.
  2. Apply f to the vertices of each facet.
  3. Remove repeated image vertices when interpreting the image as a set.
  4. Check that every resulting image set is a simplex of L.
  5. Record any dimension collapses.
  6. If an isomorphism is claimed, check vertex bijectivity and verify the inverse is simplicial.
  7. If a homeomorphism or embedding is claimed, do not infer it merely from simpliciality.

18. Worked Audit

Let K have facets abc, acd, and ade. Let L have facets pqr and prs. Define

a↦p, b↦q, c↦r, d↦s, e↦r.

  • abc maps to pqr — valid.
  • acd maps to prs — valid.
  • ade maps to psr — the same vertex set {p,r,s}, valid.

Thus the map is simplicial. It is not injective on vertices because c and e both map to r, so it cannot be a simplicial isomorphism.

19. Common Errors

  1. Checking vertices only. A vertex assignment must preserve whole simplices.
  2. Demanding dimension preservation. Simplicial maps may collapse simplices.
  3. Assuming simplicial means injective. It does not.
  4. Assuming a homeomorphism is simplicial for the chosen triangulations. It may not be.
  5. Replacing “homotopic to” with “equal to” in simplicial approximation. These are different conclusions.
  6. Ignoring the target complex. Whether a vertex rule is simplicial depends on which target simplices actually exist.

20. Knowledge Check

  1. Define a simplicial map between abstract simplicial complexes.
  2. Why may repeated image vertices occur?
  3. What must be checked to determine whether a vertex rule is simplicial?
  4. How is |f| defined using barycentric coordinates?
  5. Why is |f| continuous across shared faces?
  6. What is a simplicial isomorphism?
  7. Give an example of a simplicial map that is not a homeomorphism.
  8. Why can subdivision help turn continuous-map questions into combinatorial ones?
  9. State the finite-complex simplicial approximation theorem in the form used in this lesson.
  10. Distinguish “F=|g|” from “F≃|g|.”

21. Independent Investigation — Map Laboratory

Create two finite simplicial complexes K and L, each with at least three facets, and design three different vertex maps K→L.

  1. Make Map A simplicial and injective on vertices.
  2. Make Map B simplicial but force at least one simplex to collapse in dimension.
  3. Make Map C fail to be simplicial.
  4. For each map, audit every source facet.
  5. For A and B, describe the induced realization map qualitatively.
  6. Explain exactly why C fails.
  7. Determine whether either valid map is a simplicial isomorphism and justify the conclusion.
