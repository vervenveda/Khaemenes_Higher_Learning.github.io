# Lesson 13 — Forensic Verification Notes

## Role
Lesson 13 integrates Lessons 11 and 12 by requiring students to use subdivision to make continuous-map behavior combinatorially representable.

## Mathematical checks
PASS:
- a simplicial approximation is not treated as an arbitrary vertex choice;
- the open-star containment criterion is used as the operational compatibility test;
- a coarse interval mapping across a two-edge target path is correctly shown to require subdivision before it can be encoded simplicially;
- the subdivided interval map `a→x, m→y, b→z` sends source edges to target edges;
- barycentric subdivision of one triangle is treated using face-chain vertices and six small triangles;
- homotopy `F ≃ |g|` is never replaced by literal equality;
- simpliciality is distinguished from being an approximation to a particular continuous map;
- visual closeness is not treated as sufficient evidence.

## Scope discipline
The full proof of the simplicial approximation theorem is intentionally deferred. The lesson uses the theorem and its star criterion as established tools after Lesson 12's theorem preview.
