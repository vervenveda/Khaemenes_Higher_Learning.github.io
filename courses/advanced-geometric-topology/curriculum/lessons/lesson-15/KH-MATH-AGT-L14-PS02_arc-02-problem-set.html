Arc 02: Triangulations & Combinatorial Surfaces

Coverage: Lessons 09–13

Purpose

This problem set consolidates the complete Arc 02 chain:

simplicial complexes → stars & links → subdivision → simplicial maps → simplicial approximation

Show your reasoning. Numerical answers without the combinatorial audit that supports them are incomplete.

Directions

  • Use the nonempty-face convention adopted in Lesson 09.
  • List facets and faces carefully; do not count duplicates twice.
  • Distinguish local manifold evidence from global conclusions.
  • When discussing maps, audit complete source simplices rather than vertices alone.
  • Distinguish equality from homotopy.
  • Label theorem-based conclusions separately from direct verification when requested.

Part I — Simplices, Complexes & Realization

  1. Face closure. Let C={{a,b,c},{a,b},{a,c},{a},{b},{c}}. Is C an abstract simplicial complex? If not, identify every missing face needed to repair it.
  2. Generated complex. Let K be generated by facets abc, acd, and ade. List every vertex, edge, and triangle exactly once.
  3. Dimension. A simplex has nine vertices. Determine its dimension and explain the rule.
  4. f-vector. Compute the f-vector and Euler characteristic of the complex generated by facets abc and acd.
  5. K versus |K|. Explain the conceptual difference between an abstract simplicial complex and its geometric realization.
  6. Triangulation. State precisely what additional data/relationship is required to say that K triangulates a topological space X.

Part II — Stars, Links & Local Manifold Tests

  1. Cycle link. Suppose the triangles containing v are vab, vbc, vcd, and vda. Construct Lk(v) and classify v locally.
  2. Boundary link. Suppose the triangles containing v are vab, vbc, and vcd, with no triangle vda. Construct the link and determine whether the pattern is compatible with a boundary vertex.
  3. Three sheets. The triangles abc, abd, and abe share edge ab. Compute Lk(ab) and explain why interior points of ab are nonmanifold points.
  4. Pinched vertex. Two triangulated disks are identified at exactly one vertex. Describe the link of the identified vertex and explain the failure of the local manifold test.
  5. Purity. Give a pure 2-dimensional simplicial complex that nevertheless fails to be a 2-manifold. Explain the failure using a link.
  6. Local versus global. Construct or describe a finite complex in which every vertex has a circle link but the realization is not connected.

Part III — Subdivision & Invariance

  1. Edge subdivision. Subdivide one edge of a single triangle by inserting a vertex m. Give the new facets, f-vector, and Euler characteristic.
  2. Starring a triangle. Insert an interior vertex x into triangle abc and cone x to all three boundary edges. Compute ΔV, ΔE, ΔF, and Δχ.
  3. Barycentric subdivision. For one 2-simplex, explain why sd K has 7 vertices and 6 triangular facets. Compute the number of edges and verify χ.
  4. Face chains. Write three distinct maximal chains of nonempty faces of triangle abc and identify the corresponding triangles of sd K.
  5. False subdivision. Start with triangle abc and attach triangle cde only at c. Explain at least two reasons this is not a subdivision of the original triangle.
  6. Local preservation. Subdivide an interior edge ab shared by abc and abd. Construct the link of the new midpoint m and classify it.

Part IV — Simplicial Maps

  1. Map audit. K has facets abc and acd. L contains triangle xyz and all faces. Let a↦x, b↦y, c↦z, d↦y. Determine whether the map is simplicial and justify facet by facet.
  2. Collapse. A triangle abc maps by a↦x, b↦y, c↦y. What is the image simplex? What dimension is lost?
  3. Failure. Give a target complex L and a vertex assignment from triangle abc that fails to define a simplicial map even though every image vertex exists in L.
  4. Realization. If p=(1/4)a+(1/4)b+(1/2)c and f(a)=x, f(b)=y, f(c)=z, compute |f|(p) in barycentric form.
  5. Composition. Explain why the composition of two simplicial maps is simplicial.
  6. Isomorphism test. State what must be checked before a simplicial map may be called a simplicial isomorphism.

Part V — Simplicial Approximation

  1. Coarse interval. A source edge ab maps continuously along the target path x-y-z from x to z. Explain why a↦x, b↦z is not simplicial when xz is not an edge.
  2. Repair by subdivision. Insert m into ab and define a↦x, m↦y, b↦z. Verify the new map simplex by simplex.
  3. Star criterion. State the open-star containment condition used in Lesson 13 for a proposed simplicial approximation g.
  4. Homotopy discipline. Explain the difference between F=|g| and F≃|g|.
  5. Bad reasoning. Refute: “I chose the visually nearest target vertex for every source vertex, so the result is automatically a simplicial approximation.”
  6. Why subdivision helps. Explain conceptually why repeated barycentric subdivision can make a continuous map easier to approximate simplicially.

Part VI — Integrated Forensics

  1. Full complex audit. Let K have facets abc, acd, ade, aeb. Compute the f-vector and χ, count triangle incidences at every edge, construct each vertex link, and decide whether |K| is locally a surface with boundary.
  2. Map after refinement. Construct a finite source complex K and target L for which a desired continuous-map behavior cannot be represented simplicially on K but can be represented after one subdivision. Give all facets and the complete map audit.
  3. Invariant versus presentation. Two triangulations of a disk have different f-vectors. Explain why this does not contradict topological invariance of Euler characteristic.
  4. Homeomorphism warning. Explain why |K|≅|L| does not imply that K and L are simplicially isomorphic as originally triangulated.
  5. Proof boundary. Separate the following into direct combinatorial checks and theorem-based conclusions: (a) a proposed vertex map sends every facet to a target simplex; (b) its realization is continuous; (c) a sufficiently subdivided continuous map admits a simplicial approximation up to homotopy; (d) a listed vertex link is a 5-cycle.
  6. Synthesis. In 150–250 words, explain how Arc 02 turns topology into controlled combinatorics without confusing a combinatorial model with the underlying topological space.

Submission Checklist

  • Problems 1–36 attempted.
  • All requested facet/face lists shown.
  • Euler calculations include V, E, and F.
  • Local manifold claims cite link or incidence evidence.
  • Map claims include simplex-by-simplex audits.
  • Homotopy notation is used correctly.
  • Integrated problems distinguish direct checks from theorem use.

Accessibility

All problems can be completed using symbolic simplex lists, incidence tables, and written reasoning. Diagrams are optional. No color distinction, physical model, paid software, or external website is required.
