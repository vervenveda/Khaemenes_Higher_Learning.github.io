Arc 02 Mastery Assessment

Coverage: Lessons 09–13

This assessment uses new examples rather than copying the Lesson 14 problem set. Show enough work to make every combinatorial and topological conclusion auditable.

Suggested mastery threshold: 80%. Instructor or academy policy determines final scoring and reassessment.

Assessment Rules

  • Use the nonempty-face convention from Arc 02.
  • Count distinct simplices only once.
  • Support local-manifold claims with links or incidence data.
  • Audit simplicial maps simplex by simplex.
  • Do not replace “homotopic” by “equal.”
  • Clearly distinguish direct verification from theorem use.

Part A — Complexes & Local Structure · 24 points

  1. [6] K has facets abc, acd, cde. List all vertices and edges, compute the f-vector and Euler characteristic, and identify every edge contained in two triangles.
  2. [6] A vertex v lies in triangles vab, vbc, vcd, vde. Construct Lk(v). Is the local structure compatible with an interior vertex, a boundary vertex, or neither? Justify.
  3. [6] An edge pq belongs to triangles pqr, pqs, pqt, pqu. Compute Lk(pq) and diagnose the local topology.
  4. [6] Explain why checking only that every edge belongs to one or two triangles is not, by itself, a complete proof that a pure finite 2-complex is a surface. Name an additional local audit.

Part B — Subdivision & Invariance · 20 points

  1. [5] A quadrilateral disk is triangulated by diagonal ac, giving facets abc, acd. Insert a new vertex m in the interior of ac. Give the refined facets, f-vector, and χ.
  2. [5] Explain abstractly how vertices and r-simplices of sd K are defined using nonempty faces and strict face chains.
  3. [5] A student attaches a new triangle to an existing triangulated disk along one boundary vertex and calls the operation a subdivision. Audit the claim.
  4. [5] Explain why equal Euler characteristic before and after an arbitrary modification is insufficient to prove that the modification was a subdivision.

Part C — Simplicial Maps · 24 points

  1. [6] K has facets abc, bcd. L has facets xyz, xyw. Define a→z, b→x, c→y, d→w. Determine whether the rule is simplicial. Show both facet images.
  2. [6] Let a triangle abc map by a→x, b→x, c→x. Describe the image simplex and induced realization. Is the map necessarily a homeomorphism?
  3. [6] For p=(1/2)a+(1/3)b+(1/6)c and f(a)=x, f(b)=y, f(c)=z, write |f|(p).
  4. [6] State the difference between a simplicial map and a simplicial isomorphism. What extra property must the latter possess?

Part D — Simplicial Approximation · 20 points

  1. [5] State the open-star containment criterion used in Arc 02 for a proposed simplicial approximation g to F.
  2. [5] A single source edge must model a continuous path traversing three consecutive target edges. Explain why the original source may be too coarse and describe a suitable refinement.
  3. [5] State the simplicial approximation conclusion for a continuous map from the realization of a finite complex, using barycentric subdivision and the correct homotopy notation.
  4. [5] Refute: “If g is simplicial, then g is automatically a simplicial approximation to whichever continuous map F we happen to be studying.”

Part E — Integrated Forensic Reasoning · 12 points

  1. [6] Construct a pure finite 2-complex that is not a 2-manifold. Give its facets and prove failure using a link.
  2. [6] In 120–180 words, explain how subdivision and simplicial approximation work together to let topology use finite combinatorial data without claiming that the combinatorial presentation is unique.
