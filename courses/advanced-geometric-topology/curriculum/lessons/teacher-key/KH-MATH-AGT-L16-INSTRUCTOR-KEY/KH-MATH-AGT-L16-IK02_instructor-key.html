Answer & Scoring Key

1 · 6 points

Facets abc, acd, cde give vertices a,b,c,d,e and edges ab,bc,ac,ad,cd,ce,de. Thus (V,E,F)=(5,7,3) and χ=5−7+3=1. Edges ac and cd each occur in two triangles.

Rubric: faces/counts 3; χ 1; two-triangle edges 2.

2 · 6 points

Lk(v) has edges ab,bc,cd,de: the path a-b-c-d-e. Its realization is an interval, so the pattern is compatible with a boundary vertex.

Rubric: correct link 4; classification/reason 2.

3 · 6 points

Lk(pq) consists of four isolated vertices r,s,t,u. A surface edge has a one-point boundary link or two-point interior link. Four points diagnose a nonmanifold edge.

Rubric: link 3; expected manifold links 2; diagnosis 1.

4 · 6 points

Edge incidence alone can miss singular vertex neighborhoods. One must also audit vertex links: for a triangulated surface they should be circles at interior vertices or intervals at boundary vertices (with the course's finite pure 2-complex hypotheses).

Rubric: limitation 3; vertex-link audit 3.

5 · 5 points

Replace abc and acd by abm,bcm,amd,cmd. Vertices a,b,c,d,m: V=5. Edges ab,bc,cm,ma,md,dc,ad,bm: E=8. F=4. Hence χ=5−8+4=1.

Rubric: facets 2; f-vector 2; χ 1.

6 · 5 points

Vertices of sd K correspond to nonempty simplices of K. An r-simplex of sd K corresponds to a strict chain σ0⊂σ1⊂⋯⊂σr of nonempty simplices of K.

7 · 5 points

The attached triangle introduces points not contained in the original disk and changes the underlying polyhedron/local neighborhood. It is an attachment, not merely a refinement of existing simplices.

8 · 5 points

Euler characteristic is too coarse to certify subdivision: distinct, even nonhomeomorphic spaces can share χ. A subdivision must satisfy geometric/refinement conditions and preserve the underlying polyhedron; equality of one invariant cannot establish those conditions.

9 · 6 points

abc maps to {z,x,y}=xyz; bcd maps to {x,y,w}=xyw. Both are target facets, so the map is simplicial.

Rubric: first facet 2; second 2; conclusion 2.

10 · 6 points

The triangle maps to the single vertex {x}; its realization is the constant map to x. It is not a homeomorphism for a nontrivial triangular realization.

11 · 6 points

|f|(p)=(1/2)x+(1/3)y+(1/6)z.

12 · 6 points

A simplicial map sends source simplices to target simplices. A simplicial isomorphism must possess a simplicial inverse; equivalently here it is a vertex bijection preserving simplices in both directions.

13 · 5 points

For every source vertex v, require F(st(v)) ⊆ st(g(v)), using open stars in the relevant source and target triangulations.

14 · 5 points

One source edge can map simplicially only to one target simplex. To encode traversal of three consecutive target edges, subdivide the source edge into at least three consecutive source edges and map their successive vertices to the four successive target vertices.

15 · 5 points

In the finite-complex form used in Arc 02: after sufficiently many barycentric subdivisions of K, there exists a simplicial map g:sdⁿK→L whose realization is homotopic to F, written |g|≃F (after the standard identification of |sdⁿK| with |K|).

16 · 5 points

Simpliciality only says g respects source and target simplices. Being a simplicial approximation to a particular F additionally requires compatibility with F, such as the open-star containment criterion. The same simplicial g is not automatically an approximation to an arbitrary F.

17 · 6 points

Canonical example: facets abc,abd,abe. The complex is pure because every facet is 2-dimensional. Edge ab has link {c,d,e}, three isolated points, rather than one or two points; hence it is not a 2-manifold with boundary.

Equivalent valid counterexamples earn full credit when the link proof is correct.

18 · 6 points

Full-credit elements: subdivision refines the combinatorial presentation while preserving the realized topology; finer stars/simplices give greater resolution; simplicial approximation replaces suitable continuous-map behavior, after enough subdivision, by a simplicial map up to homotopy; triangulations are not unique; the combinatorial model must not be confused with the underlying space; direct checks must be distinguished from theorem-based conclusions.
