# W01D1 Teacher / Reviewer Key

## Guided practice

1. For `a a b b`, the learner should identify:
   - the two edge pairs,
   - the orientation relation for each pair,
   - the vertex equivalence classes induced by those pairings,
   - the quotient cell counts,
   - orientability evidence,
   - and only then a candidate surface identification.

2. With `V = 2`, `E = 3`, `F = 1`:
   `χ = 2 - 3 + 1 = 0`.

3. Euler characteristic is a homeomorphism invariant, but it is not by itself a complete invariant for closed surfaces. Orientability must also be considered in the usual classification framework.

## Independent practice checkpoints

- One face, four quotient edges, one quotient vertex gives `χ = 1 - 4 + 1 = -2`.
- The quotient map is not injective wherever distinct boundary points are identified.
- `a b a^-1 b^-1` is the standard torus word; `a b a^-1 b` is a standard Klein-bottle presentation.
- Vertex classes must be determined before `V` is known.
- A correct response to the last item may use orientability as information that χ alone does not capture.

## Proof task 1

A complete response labels the square vertices and uses both edge pairings to show that each vertex is equivalent to the next under the generated equivalence relation. The transitive closure therefore contains all four vertices, yielding one quotient 0-cell.

## Proof task 2

Expected structure:

1. If two spaces are homeomorphic, they have equal Euler characteristic.
2. The converse does not follow merely from invariance.
3. Euler characteristic records only one invariant.
4. In the classification of closed surfaces, orientability is additional information.
5. Therefore equal χ is not, in general, sufficient by itself to conclude homeomorphism.
