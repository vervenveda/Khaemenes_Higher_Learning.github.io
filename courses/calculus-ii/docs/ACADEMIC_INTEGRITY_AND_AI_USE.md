# Academic Integrity and Responsible AI Use

Calculus learning requires an inspectable reasoning trail. Submitted work must distinguish the learner's own derivation from external assistance.

## Permitted support

- reviewing definitions, examples, and prerequisite explanations;
- using calculators, graphing tools, CAD, code, or AI to explore or independently verify a result when the task permits technology;
- requesting feedback on clarity after a complete mathematical attempt;
- comparing multiple solution methods and documenting the comparison.

## Required disclosure

When a tool materially contributes to a submitted investigation or capstone, record the tool, prompt or command, output used, mathematical claim checked, and the learner's independent validation.

## Not sufficient as evidence

- copied symbolic output without derivation;
- an AI-generated explanation the learner cannot defend;
- a graph or decimal answer without setup, units, or interpretation;
- fabricated data, citations, calculations, or verification;
- undisclosed assistance on a restricted formal assessment.

Faculty should evaluate the mathematical decisions, not merely the polish of the final presentation. Oral defense and fresh parallel problems provide strong authorship evidence.
