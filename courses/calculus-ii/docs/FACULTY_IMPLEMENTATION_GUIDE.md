# Faculty Implementation Guide

## Recommended weekly rhythm

1. prerequisite retrieval and diagnostic response;
2. concept seminar and worked-example reconstruction;
3. pathway practice and representation comparison;
4. application, investigation, or error analysis;
5. mastery evidence, correction, reflection, and portfolio selection.

## Seminar expectations

Ask learners to classify structure before calculating. Require theorem hypotheses, domain restrictions, units, and interpretation. Use oral questioning to expose method choice and model assumptions rather than merely reproducing procedures.

## Differentiation

- **Foundation:** retrieval cues, smaller algebraic cases, partially completed structures, and worked-example fading.
- **Core:** standard college fluency, complete applications, and independent verification.
- **Honors / Extended:** proof, parameter analysis, unfamiliar synthesis, sharper error estimates, and alternative methods.

Pathways describe current support needs, not fixed ability groups. Learners may move between them by topic.

## Feedback and reassessment

Mark the first invalid step, not just the final answer. Require a written correction explaining why the repair is valid. Reassessment should use fresh values or a parallel task and should preserve the original evidence.

## Technology

Technology may graph, approximate, simulate, or verify. It may not replace method selection, derivation, theorem conditions, units, interpretation, or error analysis. Retain screenshots or exported data only when they support a documented mathematical claim.

## Portfolio and oral defense

A complete portfolio should include representative exact integration, numerical error, an applied integral model, a differential-equation model, parametric or polar analysis, convergence-test reasoning, a Taylor approximation with error, corrected assessments, and the final capstone. Oral defense prompts should probe validity, alternatives, sensitivity, and limitations.
