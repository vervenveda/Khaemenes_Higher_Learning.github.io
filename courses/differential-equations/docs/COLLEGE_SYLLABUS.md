# Khaemenes Higher Learning Differential Equations

## Rates of Change · Dynamic Systems · Physics · Engineering

**Course code:** KH-HL-MATH-DE1  
**Length:** 36 weeks · 180 sessions · 92 lessons  
**Credit description:** Rigorous first college course in ordinary differential equations; receiving institutions independently determine credit or placement.  
**Author:** Jennifer Pearl

## Course description

This course develops analytical, qualitative, numerical, and modelling fluency across first-order equations, physical and engineering applications, numerical solvers, second-order linear equations, oscillation and resonance, Laplace transforms, systems of differential equations, nonlinear dynamics, series solutions, boundary-value problems, Fourier methods, and a PDE bridge. Learners derive rate laws, solve or approximate them, interpret parameters and long-term behavior, verify solutions, assess uncertainty, and communicate limitations.

## Prerequisite and placement

Calculus I fluency in differentiation, integration, exponential/logarithmic functions, and trigonometry is required. Calculus II techniques, complex numbers, and basic matrix/eigenvalue methods are developed or refreshed where needed. The readiness diagnostic informs Foundation, Core, or Honors / Extended placement without excluding a prepared learner.

## Learning outcomes

Learners will be able to:

1. Translate physical, engineering, biological, and environmental rate laws into well-defined IVPs, BVPs, or systems.
2. Classify differential equations and choose defensible analytical, qualitative, transform, series, or numerical methods.
3. Solve and interpret first- and second-order linear equations and major nonlinear first-order models.
4. Analyze oscillation, damping, resonance, circuits, switching, impulses, and transfer functions.
5. Approximate solutions with Euler, Heun, RK4, and adaptive/stiff-solver concepts while reporting error and stability evidence.
6. Analyze coupled linear and nonlinear systems through eigenstructure, phase portraits, nullclines, and local stability.
7. Use power series, Frobenius methods, boundary eigenproblems, Fourier series, and separation of variables as an advanced bridge.
8. Calibrate, validate, reproduce, and defend a differential-equation model in writing and oral presentation.

- Unit mastery checks: 20%
- Thirteen investigations: 20%
- Midterm: 15%
- Comprehensive final: 15%
- Capstone and oral defense: 10%

## Technology

Technology is used for slope fields, numerical integration, phase portraits, oscillation and frequency response, transform checks, parameter estimation, and reproducible simulation. Learners must still show model derivation, method selection, conditions, units, interpretation, and verification.

## Academic integrity

Collaboration is encouraged during learning when attribution and individual write-up rules are followed. Formal assessments and oral defenses must reflect the learner's own understanding. AI, computer algebra, and numerical software may support checking or exploration only when their use is documented and independently verified.
