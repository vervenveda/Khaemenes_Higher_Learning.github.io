# Discrete Mathematics 36-Week Curriculum Map

## Unit 01 — Logic, Propositions & Valid Reasoning (Weeks 1–3)

**Essential question:** How does formal logic turn precise statements into verifiable arguments, specifications, and computational decisions?

**Investigation:** Logic Specification & Argument Validity Audit

- Week 1: Statements, Propositions and Truth Values; Logical Connectives and Compound Statements; Truth Tables and Semantic Evaluation
- Week 2: Logical Equivalence and Algebra of Propositions; Implication, Converse, Inverse and Contrapositive
- Week 3: Predicates, Quantifiers and Negation; Rules of Inference, Valid Arguments and Fallacies
## Unit 02 — Sets, Functions, Relations & Cardinality (Weeks 4–6)

**Essential question:** How do sets, functions, and relations model the objects, mappings, and structural constraints used throughout computer science?

**Investigation:** Data-Type, Relation & Function Model Portfolio

- Week 4: Set Language, Membership and Subsets; Set Operations and Identities; Power Sets, Cartesian Products and Tuples
- Week 5: Functions, Domains, Codomains and Ranges; Injective, Surjective and Bijective Functions
- Week 6: Relations and Their Properties; Equivalence Relations, Partitions, Orders and Cardinality
## Unit 03 — Proof, Induction, Recursion & Invariants (Weeks 7–9)

**Essential question:** How do proof techniques establish correctness, reveal structure, and guarantee that recursive or iterative processes behave as intended?

**Investigation:** Proof Portfolio & Algorithm-Correctness Defense

- Week 7: Definitions, Theorems and the Anatomy of Proof; Direct Proof and Constructive Reasoning; Proof by Contraposition
- Week 8: Proof by Contradiction and Counterexample; Proof by Cases, Existence and Uniqueness
- Week 9: Mathematical, Strong and Structural Induction; Recursive Definitions, Invariants and Termination
## Unit 04 — Number Theory, Modular Arithmetic & Cryptography (Weeks 10–12)

**Essential question:** How do divisibility and congruence create efficient arithmetic systems for computation, hashing, coding, and cryptography?

**Investigation:** Modular Arithmetic & Public-Key Cryptography Laboratory

- Week 10: Divisibility and the Division Algorithm; Greatest Common Divisors and the Euclidean Algorithm; Prime Numbers and Unique Factorization
- Week 11: Congruence and Modular Arithmetic; Modular Inverses and Linear Congruences
- Week 12: Chinese Remainder Theorem and Fast Exponentiation; RSA Foundations, Hashing and Check Digits
## Unit 05 — Combinatorics & Counting Principles (Weeks 13–15)

**Essential question:** How can complex configuration spaces be counted without listing every possibility?

**Investigation:** Combinatorial Design & Counting Argument Portfolio

- Week 13: Sum and Product Rules; Permutations and Ordered Arrangements; Combinations and Binomial Coefficients
- Week 14: Multisets and Combinations with Repetition; Pigeonhole Principle and Extremal Guarantees
- Week 15: Inclusion–Exclusion and Overlapping Constraints; Combinatorial Proofs and Binomial Identities
## Unit 06 — Sequences, Recurrences & Generating Methods (Weeks 16–18)

**Essential question:** How do recursive definitions and recurrences describe the time, space, and structure of discrete processes?

**Investigation:** Recurrence Model, Solution & Verification Study

- Week 16: Sequences and Recursive Definitions; Modelling with Recurrence Relations; Iteration, Substitution and First-Order Recurrences
- Week 17: Linear Homogeneous Recurrences and Characteristic Roots; Nonhomogeneous Recurrences and Particular Solutions
- Week 18: Divide-and-Conquer Recurrences and Growth; Generating Functions and Recurrence Verification
## Unit 07 — Discrete Probability & Randomized Reasoning (Weeks 19–21)

**Essential question:** How does probability quantify uncertainty in finite processes, randomized algorithms, and discrete systems?

**Investigation:** Randomized Algorithm & Discrete Probability Investigation

- Week 19: Finite Sample Spaces and Event Algebra; Conditional Probability and Bayes Reasoning; Independence and Repeated Trials
- Week 20: Discrete Random Variables and Expected Value; Variance, Indicators and Linearity of Expectation
- Week 21: Binomial and Geometric Distributions; Randomized Algorithms and the Probabilistic Method
## Unit 08 — Graph Theory Foundations & Traversal (Weeks 22–24)

**Essential question:** How do graphs model connectivity, dependency, movement, and interaction in computational systems?

**Investigation:** Graph Model, Traversal & Connectivity Investigation

- Week 22: Graph Models, Terminology and Representations; Degree, Handshaking and Structural Accounting; Walks, Trails, Paths and Cycles
- Week 23: Connectivity, Components and Cut Structure; Euler Trails and Circuits
- Week 24: Hamiltonian Paths, Cycles and Computational Difficulty; Breadth-First and Depth-First Traversal
## Unit 09 — Trees, Hierarchies & Spanning Structures (Weeks 25–27)

**Essential question:** How do trees represent hierarchy, support efficient search, and connect networks with minimal redundancy?

**Investigation:** Tree Representation & Minimum Spanning Network Project

- Week 25: Trees and Equivalent Characterizations; Rooted Trees, Levels and Traversals; Binary, Search and Expression Trees
- Week 26: Spanning Trees and Network Redundancy; Kruskal and Prim Minimum-Spanning Algorithms
- Week 27: Tree Encodings, Prüfer Sequences and Counting; Structural Induction and Recursive Tree Algorithms
## Unit 10 — Graph Algorithms, Coloring, Matching & Flow (Weeks 28–29)

**Essential question:** How do graph algorithms solve routing, scheduling, assignment, coloring, and capacity problems?

**Investigation:** Network Optimization & Constraint-Satisfaction Study

- Week 28: Shortest Paths and Dijkstra’s Algorithm; Directed Acyclic Graphs and Topological Ordering; Graph Coloring and Scheduling Constraints; Bipartite Graphs and Matchings
- Week 29: Hall’s Theorem and Augmenting Paths; Network Flow and Max-Flow Min-Cut; Planar Graphs, Euler’s Formula and Algorithmic Verification
## Unit 11 — Boolean Algebra, Circuits & Finite-State Systems (Weeks 30–31)

**Essential question:** How do Boolean algebra and finite-state models connect mathematical logic to hardware, software, and protocol behavior?

**Investigation:** Boolean Circuit & State-Machine Verification Project

- Week 30: Boolean Algebra and Duality; Logic Gates and Combinational Circuits; Normal Forms and Karnaugh-Map Minimization; Finite-State Machines and Transition Tables
- Week 31: Deterministic and Nondeterministic Automata; Regular Languages and Pattern Recognition; State Invariants, Protocol Verification and Model-Checking Foundations
## Unit 12 — Asymptotic Growth, Algorithm Analysis & Computability (Weeks 32–33)

**Essential question:** How do discrete mathematics and proof distinguish feasible algorithms, correct programs, and fundamentally undecidable problems?

**Investigation:** Algorithm Analysis & Computability Position Paper

- Week 32: Growth of Functions and Asymptotic Notation; Summations and Loop Analysis; Recurrences in Algorithm Analysis; Algorithm Correctness with Preconditions, Postconditions and Invariants
- Week 33: Countability and Diagonal Reasoning; Decidability, Recognizability and Reductions; P, NP and NP-Completeness Foundations
## Unit 13 — Discrete Models for Computing & Capstone (Weeks 34–36)

**Essential question:** How can logic, combinatorics, graphs, number theory, probability, and proof combine into a defensible computer-science model?

**Investigation:** Independent Discrete Mathematics Computing Capstone & Oral Defense

- Week 34: Set and Logic Models in Databases and Queries; Coding Theory and Hamming Distance; Parity, Check Codes and Error Correction
- Week 35: Constraint Satisfaction and SAT; Network Analysis, Centrality and Community Structure; Security, Privacy and Combinatorial Risk
- Week 36: Capstone Design: Model, Proof and Algorithm; Technical Report, Demonstration and Oral Defense
