# Khaemenes Higher Learning Introduction to Proofs — Advanced Syllabus

**Length:** 36 weeks · 180 sessions  
**Lessons:** 92 interactive lessons and 92 faculty keys  
**Author:** Jennifer Pearl

## Academic level

Designed as an advanced transition-to-proof course. It prepares learners for upper-level and graduate-preparatory mathematics but does not claim to be a graduate core course.

## Prerequisite

Algebra fluency and willingness to read and write mathematical arguments. Calculus or Discrete Mathematics is recommended but not required.

## Course outcomes

- Translate mathematical claims into explicit logical and quantified form.
- Construct direct, indirect, inductive, structural, and algorithmic proofs.
- Use definitions of sets, functions, relations, number systems, bounds, limits, and continuity precisely.
- Design counterexamples, lemma dependencies, and proof revisions.
- Write, cite, peer-review, and orally defend theorem-level work.

## Unit sequence

1. **Mathematical Language, Definitions & Claims** — Weeks 1–3
2. **Logic, Quantifiers & Negation** — Weeks 4–6
3. **Sets, Functions & Relations** — Weeks 7–9
4. **Direct Proof & Algebraic Structure** — Weeks 10–12
5. **Contraposition, Contradiction & Minimal Counterexamples** — Weeks 13–15
6. **Cases, Existence, Uniqueness & Construction** — Weeks 16–18
7. **Induction, Strong Induction & Well-Ordering** — Weeks 19–21
8. **Recursive Structures, Invariants & Algorithmic Proof** — Weeks 22–24
9. **Proofs in Number Theory** — Weeks 25–27
10. **Proofs in Real Analysis** — Weeks 28–30
11. **Proofs in Algebra, Combinatorics & Graphs** — Weeks 31–32
12. **Mathematical Writing, LaTeX, Peer Review & Research Ethics** — Weeks 33–34
13. **Independent Theorem Project & Oral Defense** — Weeks 35–36

## Evidence model

The course includes 13 investigations, 13 mastery checks, a 40-question diagnostic, 60-question midterm, 100-question final, portfolio, independent research capstone, reproducible appendix, and oral defense. Corrections and proof revision are required components of mastery.

## Technology and integrity

Software, calculators, proof assistants, plotting, numerical experiments, and AI may support exploration when disclosed. Learners remain responsible for verifying every theorem, source, proof, calculation, and citation and must be able to defend submitted work independently.
