# Khaemenes Higher Learning Linear Algebra

## Matrix Theory · Vector Spaces · Linear Transformations

**Course code:** KH-HL-MATH-LA1  
**Length:** 36 weeks · 180 sessions · 92 lessons  
**Credit description:** Rigorous first college course in linear algebra; receiving institutions independently determine credit or placement.  
**Author:** Jennifer Pearl

## Course description

This course develops computational fluency and conceptual understanding across systems of linear equations, matrix algebra, vector spaces, basis and dimension, linear transformations, determinants, eigenvalues and eigenvectors, orthogonality and least squares, symmetric matrices, singular value decomposition, numerical linear algebra, and modern applications. Learners move among symbolic, geometric, algorithmic, and applied representations while documenting proofs, residuals, reconstruction checks, assumptions, and limitations.

## Prerequisite and placement

Fluent high-school algebra is expected. Calculus is useful in selected applications but is not required for the core linear-algebra sequence. The readiness diagnostic informs Foundation, Core, or Honors / Extended placement without excluding a prepared learner.

## Learning outcomes

Learners will be able to:

1. Solve and interpret linear systems using elimination, factorization, and structural tests.
2. Compute with matrices and defend dimension, order, invertibility, and algorithm choices.
3. Analyze subspaces, independence, basis, coordinates, dimension, rank, and nullity.
4. Represent and reason about linear transformations, kernels, images, composition, and change of basis.
5. Use determinants, eigenstructure, orthogonality, least squares, spectral theory, and SVD.
6. Apply linear algebra to networks, data, graphics, coding, Markov models, and dynamical systems.
7. Evaluate numerical stability, conditioning, residuals, approximation error, and reproducibility.
8. Communicate definitions, proof, computation, applications, and limitations in writing and oral defense.

## Assessment

- Lesson work and retrieval: 20%
- Unit mastery checks: 20%
- Thirteen investigations: 20%
- Midterm: 15%
- Comprehensive final: 15%
- Capstone and oral defense: 10%

## Technology

Technology is used for visualization, row reduction, numerical verification, spectral exploration, and reproducible experiments. Learners must still show definitions, structure, dimensions, derivations, interpretation, and verification.

## Academic integrity

Collaboration is encouraged during learning when attribution and individual write-up rules are followed. Formal assessments and oral defenses must reflect the learner's own understanding. AI and computer algebra may support checking or exploration only when their use is documented and independently verified.
