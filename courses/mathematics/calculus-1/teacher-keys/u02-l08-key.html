Limits at Infinity and End Behavior

Infinite limits and limits at infinity distinguish local unbounded behavior from long-run behavior. Vertical asymptotes arise near finite inputs; horizontal or slant behavior describes what happens as the input magnitude grows. This lesson emphasizes limits at infinity and end behavior through symbolic, graphical, numerical, and contextual evidence.

Essential question

How can values near an input reveal behavior at that input—even when direct substitution fails?

Visual model

Infinite limits and limits at infinity distinguish local unbounded behavior from long-run behavior. Vertical asymptotes arise near finite inputs; horizontal or slant behavior describes what happens as the input magnitude grows.

Worked reasoning

Local behaviorTest values on each side of a denominator zero and record both sign and magnitude.
End behaviorCompare dominant terms or divide by the highest power to determine the long-run model.

Misconception check: A graph can cross a horizontal asymptote. The asymptote describes end behavior, not a forbidden output.

For f(x)=1/(x−3), identify the vertical asymptote.

Answer: x=3
Reasoning: The denominator is zero at that input and does not cancel.
2

For f(x)=(3x²+1)/(x²−4), find the horizontal asymptote.

Answer: y=3
Reasoning: Equal degrees give the ratio of leading coefficients.
3

As x→∞, evaluate 5/x.

Answer: 0
Reasoning: The denominator grows without bound.
4

Can a function cross a horizontal asymptote?

Answer: Yes
Reasoning: A horizontal asymptote describes end behavior.
5

State the right-hand limit of 1/x as x→0⁺.

Answer: +∞
Reasoning: Positive small denominators produce large positive values.
6

Distinguish a vertical from a horizontal asymptote.

Answer: Vertical describes behavior near a finite input; horizontal describes end behavior.
Reasoning: The directions of approach differ.
