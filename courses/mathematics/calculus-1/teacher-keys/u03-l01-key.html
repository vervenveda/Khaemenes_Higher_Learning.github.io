Continuity at a Point and on an Interval

Continuity at an input requires an existing function value, an existing limit, and agreement between them. Continuity on an interval supports global conclusions such as the Intermediate Value Theorem. This lesson emphasizes continuity at a point and on an interval through symbolic, graphical, numerical, and contextual evidence.

Essential question

How do continuity conditions connect local behavior, global guarantees, and model reliability?

Visual model

Continuity at an input requires an existing function value, an existing limit, and agreement between them. Continuity on an interval supports global conclusions such as the Intermediate Value Theorem.

\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=f(a)f(c)=Na<c<b

Worked reasoning

Three-condition testCheck the function value, the two-sided limit, and their equality in that order.
Interval guaranteeConfirm continuity on the full interval before invoking an existence theorem.

Misconception check: The Intermediate Value Theorem guarantees that a value occurs; it does not identify where it occurs or guarantee uniqueness.

What value of c makes f(x)=(x²−9)/(x−3) for x≠3, and f(3)=c continuous?

Answer: 6
Reasoning: The limit is x+3 at x=3, giving 6.
2

List the three conditions for continuity at x=a.

Answer: f(a) exists; lim x→a f(x) exists; the limit equals f(a).
Reasoning: All three are required.
3

Classify a finite hole in a graph.

Answer: Removable discontinuity
Reasoning: Defining the missing point appropriately can restore continuity.
4

Does a jump discontinuity have a two-sided limit?

Answer: No
Reasoning: The one-sided limits differ.
5

What does the Intermediate Value Theorem guarantee?

Answer: Every intermediate output occurs on a continuous interval.
Reasoning: Continuity connects endpoint outputs without gaps.
6

Why must continuity be checked before using IVT?

Answer: The theorem's guarantee depends on continuity.
Reasoning: A discontinuity can skip intermediate values.
