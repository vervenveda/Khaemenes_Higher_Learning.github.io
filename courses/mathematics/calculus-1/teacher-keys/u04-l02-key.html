Unit 04 · Lesson 02 · Week 10 · The Derivative from First Principles

The Difference Quotient

The derivative is the limit of average rates of change over shrinking intervals. Geometrically it is tangent slope; contextually it is instantaneous rate with output units divided by input units. This lesson emphasizes the difference quotient through symbolic, graphical, numerical, and contextual evidence.

Essential question

How does the limiting slope of secant lines define instantaneous change?

Visual model

The derivative is the limit of average rates of change over shrinking intervals. Geometrically it is tangent slope; contextually it is instantaneous rate with output units divided by input units.

f'(a)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}y-f(a)=f'(a)(x-a)

Worked reasoning

Secant-to-tangentCompute several secant slopes with smaller nonzero h-values and observe the value they approach.
First-principles derivativeExpand f(a+h), subtract f(a), factor or simplify, then take the limit only after cancellation.

Misconception check: The derivative is not the ratio f(x)/x. It is a limit of change in output divided by change in input.

Using first principles, find f'(3) for f(x)=x².

Answer: 6
Reasoning: The derivative of x² from the difference quotient is 2x; at 3 it is 6.
2

Write the tangent-line equation to y=x² at x=1.

Answer: y−1=2(x−1)
Reasoning: The point is (1,1) and the derivative is 2x.
3

What does f'(a)>0 indicate locally?

Answer: f is increasing near a.
Reasoning: A positive derivative means positive tangent slope.
4

Name a point where |x| is not differentiable.

Answer: x=0
Reasoning: The graph has a corner at the origin.
5

If position is measured in meters and time in seconds, give velocity units.

Answer: meters per second
Reasoning: Derivative units are output units divided by input units.
6

What feature of f often corresponds to a zero of f'?

Answer: A horizontal tangent or stationary point
Reasoning: Zero derivative means tangent slope zero.
