Derivatives of Inverse and Inverse Trigonometric Functions

The derivative of an inverse reverses input and output while taking the reciprocal of the original slope at the corresponding point. A zero original slope prevents a finite inverse derivative. This lesson emphasizes derivatives of inverse and inverse trigonometric functions through symbolic, graphical, numerical, and contextual evidence.

Essential question

How do nested, implicit, and inverse relationships change the way derivatives are calculated and interpreted?

Visual model

The derivative of an inverse reverses input and output while taking the reciprocal of the original slope at the corresponding point. A zero original slope prevents a finite inverse derivative.

(f^{-1})'(a)=\frac1{f'(f^{-1}(a))}

Worked reasoning

Locate paired pointFind the original input whose output is the inverse input.
Reciprocal slopeEvaluate the original derivative at that paired input, then take the reciprocal.

Misconception check: The inverse derivative is not simply 1/f'(a); the original derivative must be evaluated at f^{-1}(a).

If f(2)=5 and f'(2)=4, find (f⁻¹)'(5).

Answer: 1/4
Reasoning: Use the reciprocal of f' at the paired original input.
2

Differentiate arcsin x.

Answer: 1/√(1−x²)
Reasoning: This inverse-trig derivative holds for |x|<1.
3

Differentiate arctan x.

Answer: 1/(1+x²)
Reasoning: Standard inverse-trig derivative.
4

If f'(c)=0, what happens to the inverse derivative at f(c)?

Answer: It is not finite or may not exist.
Reasoning: The reciprocal slope would require division by zero.
5

Why is the input to f' equal to f⁻¹(a)?

Answer: The inverse input a corresponds to the original point whose output is a.
Reasoning: Inverse functions swap coordinates.
6

Differentiate arccos x.

Answer: −1/√(1−x²)
Reasoning: Arccos has the negative reciprocal-root derivative.
