Accumulation Functions and Variable Bounds

The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus connects accumulation and instantaneous rate. Differentiating an accumulation function recovers the integrand, while evaluating an antiderivative computes net accumulation. This lesson emphasizes accumulation functions and variable bounds through symbolic, graphical, numerical, and contextual evidence.

Essential question

How does the Fundamental Theorem connect instantaneous rate with accumulated change?

Visual model

The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus connects accumulation and instantaneous rate. Differentiating an accumulation function recovers the integrand, while evaluating an antiderivative computes net accumulation.

\frac{d}{dx}\int_a^x f(t)\,dt=f(x)\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=F(b)-F(a)

Worked reasoning

Variable upper boundEvaluate the integrand at the bound and multiply by the derivative of the bound when needed.
Evaluation theoremFind any antiderivative, evaluate at upper and lower bounds, and subtract.

Misconception check: For a variable lower bound, the derivative introduces a negative sign.
