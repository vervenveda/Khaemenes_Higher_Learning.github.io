Unit 13 · Lesson 06 · Week 36 · Applications of Integration & Calculus Capstone

Oral Defense, Revision & Comprehensive Synthesis

A calculus capstone integrates a question, data or constraints, a defensible model, derivative or integral analysis, validation, limitations, and communication. Revision should respond to evidence rather than merely alter presentation. This lesson emphasizes oral defense, revision & comprehensive synthesis through symbolic, graphical, numerical, and contextual evidence.

Open-AgePrintableFoundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%

Learning intentions

  • Define and use the central calculus language in oral defense, revision & comprehensive synthesis.
  • Move among symbolic, graphical, numerical, and contextual representations.
  • Show complete reasoning with correct notation, domain conditions, units, and intervals.
  • Verify a conclusion using a second method or representation.

Essential question

How can derivatives, integrals, and differential equations support a defensible analysis of a complex problem?

Worked reasoning

Model auditList variables, units, assumptions, domain, evidence, and the calculus method selected.
DefenseExplain why the method answers the question, how results were checked, and where the model should not be trusted.

Misconception check: A sophisticated calculation does not compensate for an undefined question, unsupported assumptions, or missing interpretation.

Three learning pathways

Foundation

Reconstruct definitions, annotate a worked example, and solve representative problems with guided prompts.

Core

Complete mixed representations, justify every major step, and verify exact and approximate results.

Extended

Generalize a result, analyze an error or edge case, and connect the lesson to modelling or proof-informed reasoning.

Communication and modelling

State what is known, identify the theorem or structure used, show symbolic work, interpret the result in context, and explain how the conclusion was checked. Calculator or CAD evidence must include settings, units, and a written interpretation.

Date
1

Name the first step of a calculus capstone.

Answer: Define a focused, measurable question.
Reasoning: The method must serve a clear question.
2

List three required model elements.

Answer: Variables with units, assumptions, and domain.
Reasoning: These establish meaning and limits.
3

Give one derivative-based analysis.

Answer: Rate, sensitivity, extrema, or optimization.
Reasoning: Derivatives analyze instantaneous change.
4

Give one integral-based analysis.

Answer: Accumulation, net change, area, volume, or average value.
Reasoning: Integrals analyze total effect.
5

How should technology evidence be used?

Answer: As verification with settings and interpretation recorded.
Reasoning: A screen result alone is not a mathematical argument.
6

What belongs in the oral defense?

Answer: Method choice, evidence, limitations, corrections, and conclusions.
Reasoning: The defense demonstrates understanding and ownership.
