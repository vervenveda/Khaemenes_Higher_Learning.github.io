Foundation · 12 problems

1. Evaluate lim x→2 (x²−4)/(x−2), showing the factorization.

Worked answer: For x≠2, cancel (x−2) from (x−2)(x+2)/(x−2). The limit is 4.

2. Evaluate lim x→3 (x+1). Explain why direct substitution works.

Worked answer: Polynomials are continuous; substitute 3, giving 4.

3. For f(x)=2x+1, calculate f(1.9), f(1.99), f(2.01), f(2.1). Predict lim x→2 f(x).

Worked answer: The values are 4.8, 4.98, 5.02, and 5.2. Both sides approach 5.

4. Evaluate lim x→0 sin(x)/x. State the unit requirement.

Worked answer: The standard trigonometric limit is 1 when x is measured in radians.

5. If lim x→1⁻ f(x)=2 and lim x→1⁺ f(x)=7, determine whether lim x→1 f(x) exists.

Worked answer: It does not exist: the one-sided limits disagree.

6. Evaluate lim x→4 (x²−16)/(x−4), for x≠4.

Worked answer: Factor the numerator to (x−4)(x+4); the limit is 8.

7. Evaluate lim x→∞ 5/x. Describe the horizontal behavior.

Worked answer: The ratio tends to 0 as x increases without bound; horizontal asymptote y=0.

8. For f(x)=(3x²+1)/(x²−4), find lim x→∞ f(x).

Worked answer: Divide numerator and denominator by x²; the limit is 3.

9. For f(x)=(x²−9)/(x−3), x≠3, identify the missing graph point.

Worked answer: For x≠3, f(x)=x+3. The graph has a removable hole at (3,6).

10. Let f(x)=x+2 when x<1 and f(x)=2x when x≥1. Does lim x→1 f(x) exist?

Worked answer: The left limit is 3 and the right limit is 2; the two-sided limit does not exist.

11. Explain whether f(a) must equal L whenever lim x→a f(x)=L. Give a counterexample.

Worked answer: No. Define f(x)=x+1 for x≠2, but f(2)=9. The limit is 3 while f(2)=9.

12. Evaluate lim x→0 |x| using left and right approach values.

Worked answer: From either side |x| approaches 0, so the limit is 0.

Core · 16 problems

1. Calculate lim x→3 (x²−9)/(x−3) and identify the excluded input.

Worked answer: Factor to x+3 for x≠3; the limit is 6. The original function excludes x=3.

2. Rationalize and evaluate lim x→4 [√(x+5)−3]/(x−4).

Worked answer: Multiply by the conjugate; obtain 1/[√(x+5)+3], giving 1/6.

3. Evaluate lim x→0 (x²−4x)/x, with x≠0.

Worked answer: Cancel x: x−4. The limit is −4.

4. Evaluate lim x→0 sin(5x)/x with x measured in radians.

Worked answer: Write 5[sin(5x)/(5x)]; the limit is 5.

5. Evaluate lim x→0 [1−cos(x)]/x using a trigonometric identity.

Worked answer: Rationalize: sin²(x)/[x(1+cos x)]. It tends to 0.

6. For f(x)=(x²−1)/(x−1) if x≠1 and f(1)=9, classify continuity at x=1.

Worked answer: The limit is 2 but f(1)=9, so the discontinuity is removable.

7. Compute lim x→−2 (x²+3x+2)/(x+2).

Worked answer: Factor (x+1)(x+2), cancel for x≠−2, then evaluate x+1=−1.

8. Find the horizontal asymptote of f(x)=(2x²+3)/(x²−1).

Worked answer: The same-degree leading coefficient ratio is 2/1; y=2.

9. Calculate lim x→∞ (3x²−x+1)/(2x²+5).

Worked answer: Divide by x²; the limit is 3/2.

10. Evaluate lim x→∞ x/(x²+1), explaining the dominant terms.

Worked answer: The denominator grows quadratically while the numerator grows linearly; limit 0.

11. Create a left/right table for f(x)=x² near x=2 and state its limit.

Worked answer: Example: f(1.9)=3.61, f(1.99)=3.9601, f(2.01)=4.0401, f(2.1)=4.41. Limit 4.

12. Evaluate lim x→0 tan(x)/x in radians.

Worked answer: tan(x)/x=[sin(x)/x]/cos(x); limit 1/1=1.

13. For f(x)=x² when x<0 and f(x)=x+1 when x≥0, evaluate lim x→0 f(x).

Worked answer: Left limit 0, right limit 1: the two-sided limit does not exist.

14. Evaluate lim x→∞ [7−3/x].

Worked answer: The term 3/x tends to 0, leaving 7.

15. Evaluate lim x→1 (x³−1)/(x−1) for x≠1.

Worked answer: Factor x³−1=(x−1)(x²+x+1); the limit is 3.

16. For f(x)=(x²−4)/(x−2) when x≠2 and f(2)=7, compare the limit and f(2).

Worked answer: The limit is 4, while the assigned function value is 7; discontinuous at x=2.

Extended · 12 problems

1. Evaluate lim x→0 [√(4+x)−2]/x and justify rationalization.

Worked answer: The conjugate gives 1/[√(4+x)+2], so the limit is 1/4.

2. Evaluate lim x→0 sin(3x)/sin(5x), in radians.

Worked answer: Write [sin(3x)/(3x)]/[sin(5x)/(5x)]×3/5, yielding 3/5.

3. Evaluate lim x→∞ (4x³−2x)/(2x³+7) with leading-term reasoning.

Worked answer: Divide by x³; all lower-order terms vanish. The limit is 2.

4. Determine lim x→∞ (x²+1)/(x+2), including its sign.

Worked answer: The ratio grows like x, so it tends to +∞. No finite horizontal asymptote as x→∞.

5. Test the existence of lim x→0 |x|/x using two one-sided calculations.

Worked answer: For x<0 the ratio is −1; for x>0 it is +1. The two-sided limit does not exist.

6. Calculate lim x→2 (x³−8)/(x−2) using a difference of cubes.

Worked answer: Cancel (x−2) in (x−2)(x²+2x+4); evaluate to 12.

7. Choose k so that f(x)=x²+k for x<1 and f(x)=3x for x≥1 is continuous at 1.

Worked answer: Match one-sided values: 1+k=3, so k=2.

8. Evaluate lim x→0 [e^(2x)−1]/x. Identify the underlying standard limit.

Worked answer: Substitute u=2x: 2(e^u−1)/u tends to 2.

9. Compute lim x→∞ √(x²+4)/x, carefully handling absolute value.

Worked answer: For positive x, √(x²+4)/x=√(1+4/x²), tending to +1.

10. Compute lim x→−∞ √(x²+4)/x. Explain the different sign.

Worked answer: Here x<0, so |x|/x=−1. The expression tends to −1.

11. Give a function continuous at zero but not differentiable there, and justify.

Worked answer: f(x)=|x| is continuous at 0. Left derivative −1 and right derivative +1 differ.

12. Give a function that crosses its own horizontal asymptote, and prove both claims.

Worked answer: f(x)=x/(x²+1) crosses y=0 at x=0, while lim x→±∞ f(x)=0.
