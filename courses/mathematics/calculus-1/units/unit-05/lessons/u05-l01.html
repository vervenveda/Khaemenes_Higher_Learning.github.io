Unit 05 · Lesson 01 · Week 13 · Differentiation Rules & Higher Derivatives

Constant, Power & Sum Rules

Differentiation rules compress repeated limit arguments into reliable structures. Each rule should be chosen from the algebraic form of the function and verified through units, graph behavior, or a numerical derivative. This lesson emphasizes constant, power & sum rules through symbolic, graphical, numerical, and contextual evidence.

Open-AgePrintableFoundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%

Learning intentions

  • Define and use the central calculus language in constant, power & sum rules.
  • Move among symbolic, graphical, numerical, and contextual representations.
  • Show complete reasoning with correct notation, domain conditions, units, and intervals.
  • Verify a conclusion using a second method or representation.

Essential question

How do derivative rules make complex rates of change efficient, interpretable, and verifiable?

Visual model

Differentiation rules compress repeated limit arguments into reliable structures. Each rule should be chosen from the algebraic form of the function and verified through units, graph behavior, or a numerical derivative.

\frac{d}{dx}x^n=nx^{n-1}(fg)'=f'g+fg'\left(\frac fg\right)'=\frac{f'g-fg'}{g^2}

Worked reasoning

Structure firstRewrite radicals and reciprocals as powers, identify sums, products, and quotients, then apply the matching rule.
VerificationCompare the symbolic derivative with a tangent-slope estimate at one convenient input.

Misconception check: The derivative of a product is not the product of the derivatives.

Three learning pathways

Foundation

Reconstruct definitions, annotate a worked example, and solve representative problems with guided prompts.

Core

Complete mixed representations, justify every major step, and verify exact and approximate results.

Extended

Generalize a result, analyze an error or edge case, and connect the lesson to modelling or proof-informed reasoning.

Communication and modelling

State what is known, identify the theorem or structure used, show symbolic work, interpret the result in context, and explain how the conclusion was checked. Calculator or CAD evidence must include settings, units, and a written interpretation.
