Unit 07 · Lesson 03 · Week 19 · Contextual Applications of Derivatives

Related Rates: Geometry and Measurement

Related-rates problems connect quantities that change together. An equation describes the geometric or physical relationship; differentiating with respect to time turns that relation into a rate equation. This lesson emphasizes related rates: geometry and measurement through symbolic, graphical, numerical, and contextual evidence.

Open-AgePrintableFoundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%

Learning intentions

  • Define and use the central calculus language in related rates: geometry and measurement.
  • Move among symbolic, graphical, numerical, and contextual representations.
  • Show complete reasoning with correct notation, domain conditions, units, and intervals.
  • Verify a conclusion using a second method or representation.

Essential question

How do derivatives describe motion, related quantities, sensitivity, and local approximation in authentic contexts?

Visual model

Related-rates problems connect quantities that change together. An equation describes the geometric or physical relationship; differentiating with respect to time turns that relation into a rate equation.

\frac{d}{dt}F(x,y)=0\frac{dV}{dt}=\frac{dV}{dr}\frac{dr}{dt}

Worked reasoning

Model firstDraw and label the changing quantities, write one relationship, then differentiate with respect to time.
Substitute lateInsert the instant's measurements only after obtaining the rate equation.

Misconception check: Substituting fixed measurements before differentiating can erase the variable whose rate is needed.

Three learning pathways

Foundation

Reconstruct definitions, annotate a worked example, and solve representative problems with guided prompts.

Core

Complete mixed representations, justify every major step, and verify exact and approximate results.

Extended

Generalize a result, analyze an error or edge case, and connect the lesson to modelling or proof-informed reasoning.

Communication and modelling

State what is known, identify the theorem or structure used, show symbolic work, interpret the result in context, and explain how the conclusion was checked. Calculator or CAD evidence must include settings, units, and a written interpretation.
