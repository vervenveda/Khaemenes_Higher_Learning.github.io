Unit 09 · Lesson 04 · Week 26 · Accumulation, Riemann Sums & Definite Integrals

Trapezoidal Approximation

Riemann sums approximate accumulation by adding height-times-width contributions. The sample-point rule and partition determine the estimate; refinement generally improves resolution. This lesson emphasizes trapezoidal approximation through symbolic, graphical, numerical, and contextual evidence.

Open-AgePrintableFoundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%

Learning intentions

  • Define and use the central calculus language in trapezoidal approximation.
  • Move among symbolic, graphical, numerical, and contextual representations.
  • Show complete reasoning with correct notation, domain conditions, units, and intervals.
  • Verify a conclusion using a second method or representation.

Essential question

How can infinitely refined sums measure net change and accumulated quantity?

Visual model

Riemann sums approximate accumulation by adding height-times-width contributions. The sample-point rule and partition determine the estimate; refinement generally improves resolution.

\sum_{i=1}^n f(x_i^*)\Delta x\Delta x=\frac{b-a}{n}

Worked reasoning

PartitionCompute the width, list sample inputs, evaluate heights, and sum height times width.
Bias comparisonUse increasing or decreasing behavior to predict whether left and right sums over- or underestimate.

Misconception check: The number of partition points and the number of subintervals are not the same.
