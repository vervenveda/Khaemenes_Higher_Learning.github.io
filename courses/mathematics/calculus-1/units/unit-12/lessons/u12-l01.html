Differential Equations and Slope Fields

A differential equation specifies a relationship between a quantity and its rate. Slope fields visualize local directions; initial conditions select one solution from a family. This lesson emphasizes differential equations and slope fields through symbolic, graphical, numerical, and contextual evidence.

Open-AgePrintableFoundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%

Learning intentions

  • Define and use the central calculus language in differential equations and slope fields.
  • Move among symbolic, graphical, numerical, and contextual representations.
  • Show complete reasoning with correct notation, domain conditions, units, and intervals.
  • Verify a conclusion using a second method or representation.

Essential question

How do differential equations encode change, and how do slope fields and initial conditions determine model behavior?

Visual model

A differential equation specifies a relationship between a quantity and its rate. Slope fields visualize local directions; initial conditions select one solution from a family.

\frac{dy}{dx}=F(x,y)y_{n+1}=y_n+F(x_n,y_n)\Delta x

Worked reasoning

Slope-field readingAt each point, calculate or infer the prescribed slope and follow the local direction without crossing incompatible solution paths.
Initial-value modelSeparate variables or apply Euler's method, then test the resulting function in the original equation.

Misconception check: A slope field is not the graph of dy/dx as a separate function; each segment belongs at an (x,y) location.

Three learning pathways

Foundation

Reconstruct definitions, annotate a worked example, and solve representative problems with guided prompts.

Core

Complete mixed representations, justify every major step, and verify exact and approximate results.

Extended

Generalize a result, analyze an error or edge case, and connect the lesson to modelling or proof-informed reasoning.

Communication and modelling

State what is known, identify the theorem or structure used, show symbolic work, interpret the result in context, and explain how the conclusion was checked. Calculator or CAD evidence must include settings, units, and a written interpretation.
