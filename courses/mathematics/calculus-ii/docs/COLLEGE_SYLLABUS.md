# College Syllabus — Calculus II

## Course description

Calculus II is a 36-week, 180-session second-semester college calculus course emphasizing advanced integration, applications, first-order differential equations, parametric and polar calculus, sequences and infinite series, power series, Taylor approximation, numerical methods, modelling, and mathematical communication.

## Prerequisite

Successful completion of Calculus I or equivalent demonstrated mastery, including fluent algebra and trigonometry, limits, differentiation, the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, definite integration, substitution, applications, and written justification.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, a successful learner can:

1. select and execute advanced integration methods with domain and convergence conditions;
2. build area, volume, length, surface, work, force, mass, probability, and expectation models;
3. solve and interpret separable and first-order linear differential equations and compare exact and numerical solutions;
4. analyze parametric, vector-valued, and polar curves, including slope, motion, area, and length;
5. classify sequences and series using appropriate convergence tests and quantify approximation error;
6. construct and use power and Taylor series with interval, endpoint, center, and remainder analysis;
7. communicate definitions, hypotheses, derivations, units, limitations, and independent verification;
8. defend a complete mathematical model in writing and orally.

## Learning materials

The repository contains 92 lessons and faculty keys, differentiated practice, 13 investigations, 13 mastery checks, a diagnostic, a midterm, a final, three interactive labs, local records, a portfolio system, and print-ready worksheets. A scientific calculator is useful for exploration and verification, but no commercial textbook or paid platform is required.

## Assessment

Recommended weighting: weekly coursework 25%, unit mastery 20%, investigations 20%, midterm 10%, final 15%, capstone and oral defense 10%. Faculty may adapt weighting while preserving all major evidence categories.

## Mastery and reassessment

The target is 80% with complete reasoning. Reassessment follows error analysis, correction, reteaching, and a fresh attempt. Both initial and corrected evidence should be retained.

## Accessibility and privacy

Approved accommodations should preserve the mathematical target while reducing unrelated barriers. Course records are local-first; learners should export evidence regularly.

## Credit notice

The curriculum is college-level and AP Calculus BC-informed. It is not AP-authorized, is not an accredited degree program, and does not independently guarantee institutional credit or transfer.
