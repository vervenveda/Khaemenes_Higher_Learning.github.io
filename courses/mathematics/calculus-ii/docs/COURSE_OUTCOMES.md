# Calculus II Course Outcomes

## Advanced integration

- Classify an integrand before selecting a method.
- Use integration by parts, trigonometric identities and substitutions, partial fractions, improper-integral limits, and numerical methods.
- Compare exact and approximate results and quantify error where appropriate.

## Applications and modelling

- Construct integrals for geometric, physical, engineering, and probability quantities.
- Preserve dimensions, orientation, density, pressure, and model assumptions.
- Analyze sensitivity, residuals, limitations, and reasonableness.

## Differential equations

- Interpret direction fields and equilibria.
- Apply Euler's method with an explicit step size and error discussion.
- Solve separable and first-order linear equations and apply initial conditions.
- Compare exponential, logistic, and data-informed models.

## Parametric and polar analysis

- Analyze orientation, tangent behavior, motion, area, and arc length for parametric curves.
- Interpret vector-valued position, velocity, acceleration, and speed.
- Graph and analyze polar curves, intersections, loops, area, slope, and arc length.

## Infinite processes

- Distinguish term sequences from partial-sum sequences.
- Select and justify convergence tests.
- Distinguish absolute, conditional, and divergent behavior.
- Determine radii and intervals of convergence, including endpoints.
- Build Taylor approximations and quantify remainder error.

## Mathematical practice

- Use graphical, numerical, analytical, verbal, and contextual representations.
- State hypotheses and domains before invoking theorems.
- Maintain a complete reasoning trail during technology-active work.
- Verify results independently and communicate limitations.
