Converting Between Polar and Cartesian Forms

Represent points and curves in polar form, convert between coordinate systems, and use symmetry tests to analyze polar graphs. In this lesson, the central focus is converting between polar and cartesian forms. Learners move among symbolic, graphical, numerical, and contextual evidence rather than relying on one representation alone.

Open-AgePrintableFoundation · Core · ExtendedMastery target 80%

Learning intentions

  • Interpret the defining quantities and notation used in converting between polar and cartesian forms.
  • Construct or analyze at least two representations related to converting between polar and cartesian forms.
  • Show symbolic work with correct domains, units, intervals, or angle measures.
  • Explain why the selected method is valid and check the result against another representation.

Essential question

How do polar coordinates and complex-number forms reveal rotation, magnitude, symmetry, and curves that Cartesian form can obscure?

Multiple-representation model

Represent points and curves in polar form, convert between coordinate systems, and use symmetry tests to analyze polar graphs.

Worked reasoning

Coordinate conversionFor (r,θ)=(6,2π/3), x=−3 and y=3√3.
RotationMultiplying by 2 cis(π/4) doubles magnitude and rotates every complex-plane point 45° counterclockwise.

Misconception check: A polar point has infinitely many equivalent coordinate pairs because angles may differ by full turns and radius may change sign.

Communication and reasoning
