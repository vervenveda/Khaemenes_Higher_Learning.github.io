Vector Components and Resultants

Represent magnitude and direction with vectors, resolve components, combine forces or motions, and use the dot product to analyze angle and orthogonality. In this lesson, the central focus is vector components and resultants. Learners move among symbolic, graphical, numerical, and contextual evidence rather than relying on one representation alone.

Learning intentions

  • Interpret the defining quantities and notation used in vector components and resultants.

Essential question

How do vectors and parameters represent magnitude, direction, components, paths, velocity, and two-dimensional motion?

Multiple-representation model

Represent magnitude and direction with vectors, resolve components, combine forces or motions, and use the dot product to analyze angle and orthogonality.

\|\mathbf v\|=\sqrt{v_x^2+v_y^2}\mathbf v=\langle r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta\rangle\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=\|a\|\|b\|\cos\theta

Worked reasoning

ComponentsA vector of magnitude 12 at 35° has components ⟨12 cos35°,12 sin35°⟩≈⟨9.83,6.88⟩.
InterpretationThe same Cartesian curve can be traversed in opposite directions under different parameterizations; retain the parameter interval and direction arrows.

Misconception check: Vector addition combines components; adding magnitudes alone works only in special collinear cases.

Communication and reasoning
