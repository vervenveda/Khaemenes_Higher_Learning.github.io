Ellipses and Their Geometry

Recognize, graph, and model parabolas, ellipses, and hyperbolas from standard, translated, and contextual forms. In this lesson, the central focus is ellipses and their geometry. Learners move among symbolic, graphical, numerical, and contextual evidence rather than relying on one representation alone.

Learning intentions

  • Interpret the defining quantities and notation used in ellipses and their geometry.
  • Construct or analyze at least two representations related to ellipses and their geometry.
  • Show symbolic work with correct domains, units, intervals, or angle measures.
  • Explain why the selected method is valid and check the result against another representation.

Essential question

How do matrices, systems, and conic forms organize transformations, intersections, and geometric models?

Worked reasoning

Standard form(x−2)²/25+(y+1)²/9=1 is an ellipse centered at (2,−1) with horizontal semi-major axis 5.
Geometric meaningFor a parabola, every point is equidistant from the focus and directrix; the parameter p determines both orientation and focal distance.

Misconception check: The denominator under each squared term identifies axis length, while the signs distinguish ellipses from hyperbolas.
