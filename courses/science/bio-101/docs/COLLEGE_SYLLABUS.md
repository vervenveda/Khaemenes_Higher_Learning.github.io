# BIO 101 — General Biology I · Collegiate Syllabus

**Khaemenes Higher Learning Science Department**  
**Length:** 36 weeks · 180 sessions · 92 planned lessons  
**Units:** 13  
**Investigations:** 13  
**Mastery target:** 80%  
**Pathways:** Foundation · Core · Honors / Extended

## Course description
BIO 101 is a first-semester college-level general biology course emphasizing molecular and cellular biology, genetics, biotechnology, evolution, ecology, physiology, experimental design, quantitative analysis, scientific communication, and laboratory evidence.

## Prerequisite guidance
High-school biology or equivalent readiness is recommended. Foundational chemistry, algebra, graph interpretation, proportional reasoning, and scientific writing are useful prerequisites. The readiness diagnostic identifies support needs but does not deny access.

## Weekly rhythm
A typical five-session week should include:
1. concept and mechanism,
2. model/reading and vocabulary,
3. quantitative/data evidence,
4. laboratory/investigation or applied biological problem,
5. synthesis, formative check, portfolio/evidence work.

## Assessment
See `ASSESSMENT_MODEL.md`. Official unit mastery, midterm, final, laboratory review, and capstone evidence remain distinct from formative vocabulary or practice activities.

## Laboratory
Every unit contains an evidence-bearing investigation. The course accepts clearly labeled wet-lab, microscopy, field, simulation, or dataset modes where scientifically appropriate; one mode must never be misrepresented as another.

## Academic integrity
Protected assessment content is not part of public review. Sources must be cited. AI or computational tools may support planning, checking, visualization, or revision when disclosed, but may not replace required learner reasoning, raw evidence, or oral defense.

## Credit boundary
BIO 101 is designed to meet rigorous first-semester college biology expectations. Khaemenes Higher Learning does not independently guarantee accredited college credit, transfer, licensure, or institutional placement. Those decisions remain with receiving institutions.
