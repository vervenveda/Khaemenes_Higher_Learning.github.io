# BIO 101 Course Outcomes

By the end of BIO 101, a successful learner should be able to:

1. Design controlled biological investigations and distinguish variables, controls, replication, uncertainty, and confounding factors.
2. Analyze biological data quantitatively using graphs, rates, proportions, probability, simple statistical reasoning, and evidence limits.
3. Explain how molecular structure and interactions produce biological function.
4. Explain cell organization, membrane transport, energy transformation, and metabolic regulation across scales.
5. Model cell signaling, feedback, cell-cycle control, and homeostatic regulation.
6. Use meiosis, probability, pedigrees, and statistical comparison to reason about inheritance.
7. Explain DNA replication, transcription, translation, mutation, and gene regulation using molecular evidence.
8. Evaluate biotechnology and genomic evidence while recognizing contamination, control, privacy, consent, and ethical limits.
9. Analyze evolutionary change using population genetics, selection, drift, gene flow, speciation, and phylogenetic evidence.
10. Model ecological energy flow, population dynamics, biogeochemical cycles, biodiversity, and resilience.
11. Compare physiological systems and explain how feedback and exchange maintain organismal function.
12. Maintain a traceable laboratory portfolio with raw evidence, calculations, figures, uncertainty, limitations, and revisions.
13. Design and defend an independent biological investigation whose claims remain proportional to the evidence.

## Scientific communication standard
Learners should distinguish observation from inference, correlation from causation, model from reality, and evidence strength from confidence rhetoric. Citations, figure labels, units, uncertainty, negative/null results, and limitations are part of the scientific product rather than optional decoration.
