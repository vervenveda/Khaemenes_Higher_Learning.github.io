# BIO 101 Laboratory & Investigation Spine

BIO 101 requires **13 evidence-bearing investigations**, one aligned to each course unit. Laboratories may use safe household materials, conventional laboratory materials, simulations, public datasets, microscopy images, or field observations as appropriate, but every investigation must preserve the same scientific evidence standard.

## Unit 01 · Readiness Investigation & Evidence Defense
**Weeks:** 1, 2  
**Mode:** guided inquiry  
**Driving question:** How do measurement choices, controls, replication, and uncertainty affect the strength of a biological claim?

Required evidence:
- risk assessment
- raw measurement table
- graph
- uncertainty statement
- CER
- limitations

## Unit 02 · Structure–Function Molecular Evidence Laboratory
**Weeks:** 3, 4, 5  
**Mode:** comparative wet-lab / simulation-ready  
**Driving question:** How do molecular properties predict observable biological behavior?

Required evidence:
- controlled comparison
- molecular model
- qualitative/quantitative observations
- structure-function explanation

## Unit 03 · Microscopy, Membrane Transport & Scale Laboratory
**Weeks:** 6, 7, 8  
**Mode:** microscopy / virtual microscopy / diffusion investigation  
**Driving question:** How do scale, membrane properties, and concentration gradients constrain cellular exchange?

Required evidence:
- microscopy evidence
- scale calculation
- transport data
- model revision

## Unit 04 · Enzyme Kinetics & Metabolic Rate Laboratory
**Weeks:** 9, 10, 11  
**Mode:** quantitative inquiry  
**Driving question:** How do environmental conditions alter enzyme-mediated reaction rates?

Required evidence:
- rate data
- replicates
- graph/model
- uncertainty
- mechanistic claim

## Unit 05 · Cellular Energy & Gas-Exchange Investigation
**Weeks:** 12, 13, 14  
**Mode:** systems investigation  
**Driving question:** How can gas exchange or another proxy test models of photosynthesis and respiration?

Required evidence:
- system boundary
- rate proxy
- carbon/energy accounting
- limitations

## Unit 06 · Signaling, Feedback & Cell-Cycle Model Investigation
**Weeks:** 15, 16, 17  
**Mode:** data/model inquiry  
**Driving question:** How can perturbation evidence distinguish signaling, feedback, and cell-cycle control models?

Required evidence:
- pathway model
- perturbation evidence
- feedback analysis
- model boundary

## Unit 07 · Inheritance Probability & Pedigree Evidence Laboratory
**Weeks:** 18, 19, 20  
**Mode:** quantitative genetics  
**Driving question:** How well do observed inheritance outcomes agree with probabilistic genetic models?

Required evidence:
- probability model
- pedigree
- observed/expected table
- chi-square reasoning
- interpretation

## Unit 08 · Gene Expression & Regulation Evidence Laboratory
**Weeks:** 21, 22, 23  
**Mode:** molecular data inquiry  
**Driving question:** What evidence supports a claim about gene expression or regulation?

Required evidence:
- sequence/expression evidence
- regulatory model
- comparison
- alternative explanation

## Unit 09 · PCR, Electrophoresis & Genomic Evidence Laboratory
**Weeks:** 24, 25, 26  
**Mode:** biotechnology investigation  
**Driving question:** How can molecular separation or amplification data support a bounded genomic inference?

Required evidence:
- controls
- band/sequence evidence
- chain of custody
- contamination check
- ethical boundary

## Unit 10 · Population Change & Phylogenetic Evidence Laboratory
**Weeks:** 27, 28, 29  
**Mode:** population genetics / phylogeny  
**Driving question:** How do allele-frequency data and comparative evidence support evolutionary inference?

Required evidence:
- frequency calculations
- population model
- phylogenetic evidence
- uncertainty
- inference limits

## Unit 11 · Ecosystem Systems & Population Data Laboratory
**Weeks:** 30, 31, 32  
**Mode:** ecological data investigation  
**Driving question:** How do population dynamics, energy transfer, and nutrient cycling interact in an ecosystem model?

Required evidence:
- time-series or field data
- population model
- energy/nutrient flow
- systems feedback

## Unit 12 · Comparative Physiology & Homeostatic Systems Investigation
**Weeks:** 33, 34  
**Mode:** comparative systems inquiry  
**Driving question:** How do organisms maintain internal function under changing environmental demands?

Required evidence:
- physiological dataset
- feedback model
- comparative analysis
- system limitation

## Unit 13 · BIO 101 Independent Research Capstone & Oral Defense
**Weeks:** 35, 36  
**Mode:** independent investigation  
**Driving question:** Can the learner design and defend a reproducible biological investigation with claims proportional to evidence?

Required evidence:
- research question
- source register
- method
- raw data
- analysis
- figures
- uncertainty
- limitations
- revision history
- oral defense


## Laboratory-credit boundary
A simulation, dataset, or remote activity may support scientific inquiry when it requires genuine prediction, analysis, model evaluation, and evidence. It must not be mislabeled as hands-on wet-lab work. Faculty/parent review should identify the mode actually completed.
