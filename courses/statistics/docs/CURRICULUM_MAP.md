# Statistics 36-Week Curriculum Map

## Unit 01 — Statistical Problem Solving, Data Ethics & Measurement (Weeks 1–3)

**Essential question:** How do strong questions, trustworthy measurements, and ethical decisions turn data into defensible evidence?

**Investigation:** Data Provenance, Ethics & Investigative Question Audit

- Week 1: Statistics as an Evidence Discipline; Cases, Variables, Populations and Samples; Categorical, Quantitative and Time-Indexed Data
- Week 2: Measurement, Operational Definitions and Validity; Data Quality, Missingness and Bias
- Week 3: Ethics, Privacy, Consent and Responsible Use; Reproducible Statistical Communication
## Unit 02 — Describing One-Variable Distributions (Weeks 4–6)

**Essential question:** How do graphs and numerical summaries reveal distribution shape, center, spread, unusual values, and context?

**Investigation:** Distribution Portrait & Resistant-Summary Investigation

- Week 4: Frequency Tables, Dot Plots and Histograms; Shape, Modality, Skewness and Unusual Features; Mean, Median and Resistant Center
- Week 5: Range, Quartiles, IQR and Box Plots; Variance and Standard Deviation
- Week 6: Standard Scores and Relative Standing; Transformations and Comparing Distributions
## Unit 03 — Relationships, Correlation & Regression (Weeks 7–9)

**Essential question:** How can statistical models describe association, quantify uncertainty, diagnose error, and avoid causal overreach?

**Investigation:** Association, Prediction & Residual Analysis Portfolio

- Week 7: Scatterplots and Association; Pearson Correlation and Standardized Association; Least-Squares Regression Line
- Week 8: Residuals and Model Diagnostics; R², Prediction and Extrapolation
- Week 9: Transformations, Nonlinearity and Influential Points; Categorical Association, Confounding and Causal Claims
## Unit 04 — Study Design, Sampling & Experiments (Weeks 10–12)

**Essential question:** How do sampling, randomization, control, and design determine what conclusions data can support?

**Investigation:** Survey and Experiment Design Laboratory

- Week 10: Sampling Frames and Simple Random Samples; Stratified, Cluster and Systematic Sampling; Survey Error, Nonresponse and Question Wording
- Week 11: Observational Studies and Confounding; Randomized Experiments, Control and Replication
- Week 12: Blocking, Matching, Blinding and Placebos; Simulation-Based Design and Randomization Logic
## Unit 05 — Probability Foundations & Conditional Reasoning (Weeks 13–15)

**Essential question:** How do probability rules, conditioning, independence, and simulation quantify uncertainty?

**Investigation:** Probability Model & Bayesian Updating Investigation

- Week 13: Sample Spaces, Events and Complements; Addition Rule and Inclusion–Exclusion; Conditional Probability and Tree Diagrams
- Week 14: Independence and Multiplication Rules; Bayes Theorem and Diagnostic Reasoning
- Week 15: Counting, Permutations and Combinations; Monte Carlo Simulation and Probability Validation
## Unit 06 — Random Variables & Discrete Distributions (Weeks 16–18)

**Essential question:** How do random variables and probability distributions connect uncertain outcomes to expected value, variation, and decisions?

**Investigation:** Discrete Distribution & Expected-Value Modelling Project

- Week 16: Random Variables and Probability Mass Functions; Expected Value as Long-Run Balance; Variance, Standard Deviation and Linear Transformations
- Week 17: Binomial Distribution; Geometric and Waiting-Time Models
- Week 18: Poisson Counts and Rate Models; Covariance, Sums and Decision Applications
## Unit 07 — Continuous Distributions & Sampling Distributions (Weeks 19–21)

**Essential question:** How do density models and repeated sampling explain normal approximation, standard error, and the central limit theorem?

**Investigation:** Sampling Distribution & Central Limit Simulation

- Week 19: Continuous Random Variables and Density Curves; Normal Models and the Empirical Rule; Standard Normal Probabilities and Percentiles
- Week 20: Sampling Distribution of a Proportion; Sampling Distribution of a Mean
- Week 21: Central Limit Theorem and Normal Approximation; t Distributions and Bootstrap Intuition
## Unit 08 — Estimation & Confidence Intervals (Weeks 22–24)

**Essential question:** How do confidence intervals combine an estimate, standard error, confidence level, and design assumptions?

**Investigation:** Interval Estimation & Margin-of-Error Portfolio

- Week 22: Confidence-Interval Logic and Interpretation; One-Proportion Confidence Intervals; One-Mean t Intervals
- Week 23: Two-Proportion Intervals; Two-Mean and Paired Intervals
- Week 24: Bootstrap Confidence Intervals; Margin of Error, Sample Size and Practical Precision
## Unit 09 — Hypothesis Testing & Decision Frameworks (Weeks 25–27)

**Essential question:** How do null models, test statistics, p-values, errors, power, and effect sizes support disciplined decisions?

**Investigation:** Inference Decision Memo & Error Analysis

- Week 25: Logic of Significance Tests; Errors, Power and Study Planning; One-Proportion and One-Mean Tests
- Week 26: Two-Proportion and Two-Mean Tests; Paired Tests and Within-Subject Designs
- Week 27: Randomization and Permutation Tests; Practical Significance, Multiplicity and Evidence Quality
## Unit 10 — Categorical Data, Chi-Square & ANOVA (Weeks 28–29)

**Essential question:** How do categorical and multi-group methods compare observed variation with variation expected under a null model?

**Investigation:** Contingency Table & Group-Comparison Investigation

- Week 28: Chi-Square Goodness of Fit; Chi-Square Homogeneity; Chi-Square Independence; Standardized Residuals and Effect Size
- Week 29: One-Way ANOVA and the F Ratio; ANOVA Assumptions, Diagnostics and Robustness; Multiple Comparisons and Planned Contrasts
## Unit 11 — Multiple Regression & Model Diagnostics (Weeks 30–31)

**Essential question:** How do multiple predictors, interactions, diagnostics, and validation improve prediction without disguising uncertainty or causality?

**Investigation:** Multivariable Model Audit & Prediction Report

- Week 30: Multiple Regression and Partial Effects; Categorical Predictors and Indicator Coding; Interactions and Nonparallel Effects; Residuals, Influence and Collinearity
- Week 31: Model Selection and Adjusted Fit; Prediction Intervals and Cross-Validation; Regression Communication and Causal Limits
## Unit 12 — Resampling, Nonparametric Methods & Bayesian Foundations (Weeks 32–33)

**Essential question:** How do resampling, rank-based methods, and Bayesian updating extend inference when classical assumptions or interpretations are limiting?

**Investigation:** Resampling & Bayesian Evidence Laboratory

- Week 32: Bootstrap Distributions and Standard Errors; Permutation Tests and Exchangeability; Sign and Rank-Based Methods; Bayesian Updating: Prior, Likelihood and Posterior
- Week 33: Bayesian Estimation and Credible Intervals; Posterior Predictive Checks and Model Criticism; Frequentist and Bayesian Evidence in Context
## Unit 13 — Data Science, Reproducibility & Capstone (Weeks 34–36)

**Essential question:** How can a statistical investigation become an auditable, ethical, reproducible argument that withstands technical and public scrutiny?

**Investigation:** Independent Statistical Investigation, Technical Report & Oral Defense

- Week 34: Data Cleaning, Tidy Structure and Metadata; Missing Data, Imputation and Sensitivity; Visualization Design and Statistical Storytelling
- Week 35: Simulation, Monte Carlo and Computational Experiments; Reproducible Workflows, Versioning and Audit Trails; Fairness, Responsible AI and Statistical Harm
- Week 36: Capstone Question, Design and Evidence Plan; Technical Report, Oral Defense and Advanced Statistics Bridge
