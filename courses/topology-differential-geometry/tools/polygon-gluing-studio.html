Polygon Gluing & Surface Classification

Discover how identifying pairs of polygon edges produces closed surfaces. Compute vertex equivalence classes, the Euler characteristic, orientability and genus. This is an exact finite combinatorial model, not a pictorial proof.

Choose an edge word

Every edge name must appear exactly twice. A capital letter denotes inverse boundary direction. Use 4, 6, 8 or 10 letters.

Boundary polygon

Edges are arranged counterclockwise. Letters indicate which edges are glued; uppercase letters reverse the boundary direction relative to lowercase.

Reasoning workshop

  1. List the polygon vertices and write every identification induced by the paired edges.
  2. Count the resulting vertex classes V, the paired edges E and the single face F=1.
  3. Compute χ=V−E+F and compare it to the displayed result.
  4. Decide orientability by whether any pair has the same boundary orientation; then derive genus from χ.
  5. Explain why the torus and Klein bottle can have the same Euler characteristic yet not be homeomorphic.

Instructor evidence: Require the edge-pairing equivalence classes, a proof of χ, an orientability argument and a limitation statement. A computed value alone does not demonstrate mastery.
